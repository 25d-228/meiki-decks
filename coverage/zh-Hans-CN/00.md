# Mandarin 00 — Sound and character-input foundation

## Authority and scope

Standard Mandarin/Putonghua; Simplified Chinese; no assumed Hanzi or Japanese Kanji knowledge.
Issue #129 and its guidance-excerpts comment supply the authority. The source is the human's
`zh(2).md` (41,971 bytes; SHA-256
`907e9bae2f5a1657ef229a75340d2f5a270fc91ef33436b139686e5c3c971795`).
That identity belongs to the full upload, not the selected excerpts available to this executor.
The excerpts are not an independently verified current examination syllabus.

Four bounded practice units separate first exchanges, initials, finals and connected pronunciation.
Each has one independently authored and edited 25-card slice: 100 planned cards, zh-00-0001–0100.
This allocation reflects the four practice units and the issue's minimum slice size, not a claim
that the guide's 100-word benchmark requires 100 cards. Review may retire or revise an item rather
than pad the inventory. Final cards, targets, lexical entries and characters are counted separately.

## Explicit Meiki adaptation

The future full recording cues retrieval of a Simplified-character word or useful chunk. Existing
`reading` annotations provide contextual, tone-marked Pinyin for that hidden span only. Pinyin is
not a typed answer, spoken sentence text, or a new front-side control. The guide permits early
visible Pinyin in other study activities. Lexical tones are written consistently; short notes
explain surface changes only when relevant. Neutral syllables have no tone mark or numeral.
Word boundaries in Pinyin do not license inserting spaces between Chinese characters.

This card mode does not assess pronunciation, isolated-tone accuracy, spontaneous dialogue,
handwriting, teacher correction, multiple-speaker listening, or the guide's timed mastery gates.
The 40–60-character and approximately 100-word/chunk figures are learner benchmarks, not quotas
or achievements certified by this deck. Fuller Band 1 grammar belongs to Stage 01.

## Finite starter support

The following small set is explicitly pre-taught support, not presumed prior Chinese knowledge.
Its meanings and spoken forms need orientation before the first card. Characters are learned
inside these words; the list is not a radical or isolated-character memorization prerequisite.

- People/reference: 我 (I), 你 (you), 您 (polite you), 他 (he), 她 (she), 我们 (we),
  老师 (teacher), 同学 (classmate), 朋友 (friend), 这 (this), 那 (that).
- Basic predicates: 是 (be), 有 (have), 在 (be at), 要 (want), 来 (come), 去 (go),
  看 (look), 听 (listen), 说 (say), 读 (read), 写 (write), 吃 (eat), 喝 (drink),
  叫 (be called), 好 (good), 对 (correct), 会 (can, learned ability).
- Cue support: 请 (please), 不 (not), 没有 (not have), 的 (possessive), 吗 (yes/no question),
  呢 (return question), 什么 (what), 谁 (who), 哪儿 (where), 怎么 (how),
  这里 (here), 那里 (there), 现在 (now), 今天 (today), 很 (adjective support),
  也 (also), 都 (all), 再 (again), 一 (one), 两 (two), 个 (general classifier),
  人 (person), 字 (written character), 水 (water), 书 (book), 茶 (tea), 饭 (meal).
- Frames: 我是…；我叫…；这是…；那是…；…在这里/那里；我有/没有…；
  我要…；你要…吗？；请…；…怎么说/读/写？；…是什么？；…是谁？；
  你去哪儿？；…很好；…对吗？；你呢？; ordinary short replies within a coherent exchange.

These frames are bounded support, not coverage of the full Band 1 grammar list. No productive
tense/aspect lessons, comparisons, result complements, 把/被, relative clauses, literary forms,
idiom catalogues, full number/date/classifier tables or unintroduced modal constructions.
An author may introduce a new useful word as its card's target. Other new support must be declared
in that slice's finite support list and reviewed before acceptance. Later targets may be reused as
support only after introduction; earlier cards cannot depend on a later card's vocabulary.

## Slice specifications

### S01 — First exchanges and lexical tone orientation

- IDs: zh-00-0001–zh-00-0025 (25).
- Guide anchors: 3; 262–292 (four tones and neutral); 304–318; 891–927; 1167.
- Sound burden: useful words/chunks exemplifying each lexical tone, contrast of second/third
  tones, and ordinary neutral syllables; no artificial syllable or tone-number grid.
- Functions/word domains: greeting, leave-taking, thanks, apology, introduction, identifying a
  person or item, teacher-directed attention, a basic need. Vary the communicative task rather
  than repeating one noun-substitution frame.
- Frames: finite starter support only, with explicitly declared lexical additions if necessary.
- Input: retrieve a complete word/chunk in characters, not an isolated spelling letter;
  start contextual word-versus-character awareness without a radical lesson.
- Register: ordinary peer exchanges and polite classroom encounters; relationship explicit.
- Prerequisites: starter orientation above. Earlier S01 targets may support later S01 cards.
- Direct alignment: the hidden span itself exemplifies the declared lexical tone(s) or neutral
  syllable and performs the recorded social/basic retrieval task.
- Exclusions: phonetic explanations inside the spoken cue, complete non-fixed propositions,
  greeting inventories enlarged with names, and fuller Band 1 instruction.

### S02 — Initials in classroom and everyday action

- IDs: zh-00-0026–zh-00-0050 (25).
- Guide anchors: 262–292; 304–318; 891–927; 1017–1034.
- Sound burden: j/q/x versus zh/ch/sh, z/c/s versus zh/ch/sh, Mandarin r, and the i spellings
  after z/c/s and zh/ch/sh/r. Give genuine useful examples of every group; not every group
  is a minimal pair. Avoid a seven-column initial table or arbitrary tone-pair filler.
- Functions/word domains: following simple classroom directions, washing/eating/drinking,
  locating basic facilities, asking for repetition, simple identification and immediate needs.
- Frames: starter frames and accepted S01 support; declare any additional supporting words.
- Input/register: complete Simplified words, standard Pinyin, normal respectful classroom and
  informal practical exchanges; no English-letter reading of Pinyin.
- Prerequisites: starter orientation and S01; earlier S02 targets only within S02.
- Direct alignment: the target contains the named initial or relevant i syllable; a sound only
  in visible support does not count. Record contextual meaning as well as sound.
- Exclusions: rare lexical minimal-pair hunting, retroflex imitation of a dialect, lexical
  substitution blocks, arbitrary target enlargement and unintroduced Stage 01 grammar.

### S03 — Finals and contextual character selection

- IDs: zh-00-0051–zh-00-0075 (25).
- Guide anchors: 262–292; 891–927; 940–951; 1017–1034; 1167.
- Sound burden: ü/u; e/o/uo/ou; an/ang, en/eng, in/ing, ian/iang, uan/uang. Cover both sides
  of each contrast through natural useful words, allowing one word to demonstrate more than
  one property. No claim that all example pairs are minimal pairs or exhaustive finals.
- Functions/word domains: simple food/drink requests, finding a place or item, classroom
  confirmation, an immediate state/need, identifying an everyday person/object.
- Frames: starter frames plus accepted S01–S02; declare finite added support, not a new
  grammar inventory. Avoid templates distinguished only by an object or place.
- Input/register: contextual selection of characters; distinguish plausible homophones with
  meaning-bearing visible context; standard spelling, ü where required, plain Putonghua.
- Prerequisites: starter orientation and S01–S02; earlier S03 targets only within S03.
- Direct alignment: the hidden complete word realizes its named final(s); do not isolate
  a bound character or target an incidental modifier to avoid a duplicate.
- Exclusions: artificial rare words for a sound grid, isolated radicals, specialist advice,
  later grammar and unmarked alternate dialectal readings.

### S04 — Connected tones and integrated sound-to-character retrieval

- IDs: zh-00-0076–zh-00-0100 (25).
- Guide anchors: 262–292; 304–318; 389/453/842 (distinct Pinyin-support activities);
  844–853 (real tone combinations); 891–927; 1017–1034; 1167.
- Sound burden: common real disyllabic tone combinations, partial third tone, third-tone
  sandhi, 一 and 不 surface changes, neutral/weak syllables and short phrase grouping.
  Introduce connected-tone effects, not an exhaustive all-combinations drill.
- Functions/word domains: requesting repetition/clarification, a small quantity/basic need,
  a courteous response, a brief classroom or social exchange, contextual homophone selection.
- Frames: starter support and accepted S01–S03; explicitly declare any new finite support.
- Input/register: contextual Simplified-word retrieval; lexical-tone Pinyin plus concise
  surface notes where the sound effect is the burden; ordinary relationship-appropriate speech.
- Prerequisites: starter orientation and S01–S03; earlier S04 targets only within S04.
- Direct alignment: the target contains the sound sequence or word whose context causes the
  named change, with licensing context visible. Phrase grouping is illustrated by a useful
  compact span, never by hiding a long proposition or an entire exchange.
- Exclusions: arbitrary enlargements of previously used targets, mechanical number/classifier
  tables, claims of audio/oral accuracy without recordings, and productive Stage 01 lessons.
