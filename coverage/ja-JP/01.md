# Japanese 01 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

Japanese 01 builds a complete N5/A1 operating foundation in contemporary
standard Tokyo Japanese. Japanese 00 is the pronunciation, kana, input, and
survival-language prerequisite. This stage plans 1,000 cards in twenty-five
independent 40-card slices. Completion depends on the inventories and
contrasts below; reaching card 1,000 without satisfying them is not completion.

The stage-level lexical inventory assigns forty production targets to each
slice. The resulting 1,000 words, collocations, inflected forms, and genuine
chunks fall within the requested 900–1,200 range. Inflectional practice is
counted as grammar coverage, not as additional lexical breadth; supporting
words in accepted sentences supply the remaining lexical breadth when a slice
focuses on forms. Every accepted primary target is recorded cumulatively so a
later slice cannot use duplication as padding.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Topic and focus with `は` and `が`; objects with `を`.
- Destination and time with `に` and `へ`; action location with `で`.
- Accompaniment and introductory quotation with `と`; possession and noun
  modification with `の`; inclusion with `も`; source and endpoint with
  `から` and `まで`.
- Natural omission of recoverable arguments and ordinary time–place–manner order.
- `です`, `ではありません`, and conversational polite `じゃありません`;
  noun and `な`-adjective past forms; questions with `か`, `ね`, and `よ`.
- Demonstrative and question systems: `これ/それ/あれ/どれ`,
  `この/その/あの/どの`, `ここ/そこ/あそこ/どこ`, `だれ`, `なに/なん`,
  `いつ`, `どう`, and `どうして`.
- Practical recognition of the three verb groups; nonpast and past,
  affirmative and negative; `する` and `来る`; polite forms as baseline.
- Frequency, duration, time spans, and routine sequencing.
- `い`- and `な`-adjective present, past, affirmative, and negative forms;
  noun modification and connected descriptions.
- Natural `が` with `好き`, `嫌い`, `上手`, and `下手`.
- Accurate `て`-form formation; action sequences; requests, permission,
  prohibition, ongoing actions, common resultant states, and limited `〜てから`.
- Nonpast and past short forms, affirmative and negative; plain forms before
  `と思います`; introductory reporting with `と言っていました`;
  `〜ないでください`; relationship-based polite/plain choice.
- Invitations with `〜ませんか`; proposals and offers with `〜ましょう` and
  `〜ましょうか`; verb-stem purpose with `に行く/来る`; desire with `〜たい`.
- Simple action lists with `〜たり〜たりする`; experience with
  `〜たことがある`; comparison with `より`, `ほうが`, and superlatives.
- Beginner plans and intention; reason `から`; introductory explanatory
  `んです`; limited high-value advice and condition chunks.
- Counters, quantities, prices, dates, time, age, frequency, and duration.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Identify oneself and others; discuss nationality, language, study, and work.
- Describe family, people, possessions, homes, and where people or things are.
- Tell time and dates; discuss routines, schedules, frequency, and duration.
- Manage classroom work, questions, borrowing, communication, and study plans.
- Discuss food and preferences; order, shop, pay, compare, and use services.
- Navigate a city, use transport, plan travel, and recount a trip.
- Describe weather, seasons, appearance, condition, and evaluations.
- Discuss hobbies, media, sports, skills, likes, and dislikes.
- Describe present and past events and sequence actions.
- Request, permit, prohibit, offer help, invite, propose, and make plans.
- Report thoughts and speech with relationship-appropriate register.
- Explain reasons, ask for clarification, compare options, and give basic advice.
- Describe symptoms and obtain routine or urgent help.

## 3. Core lexical domains

- Identity, countries, languages, study, occupations, and workplaces.
- Family, relationships, people, age, appearance, and personal information.
- Home, rooms, furniture, possessions, chores, and household actions.
- School facilities, materials, courses, learning actions, and communication.
- Food, drink, cooking, restaurants, preferences, and meal service.
- Shops, money, prices, clothing, sizes, colors, and everyday services.
- City places, directions, vehicles, stations, tickets, travel, and lodging.
- Calendar, clock time, schedules, invitations, frequency, and duration.
- Weather, seasons, nature, outings, hobbies, sports, entertainment, and media.
- Body, common symptoms, health care, medicine, advice, safety, and help.
- Past events, vacations, plans, opinions, reasons, comparisons, and explanations.

## 4. Collocations and chunks

The inventory favors reusable combinations such as `日本語を勉強する`,
`七時に起きる`, `学校へ行く`, `家で料理する`, `質問に答える`,
`予約をする`, `電車に乗る`, `薬を飲む`, and complete interactional chunks.
Mechanical substitutions do not create new coverage. A grammar form counts as
a primary target only when its accepted sentence has a distinct communicative use.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- Normal beginner orthography with learned kanji, kana endings, and katakana loans.
- A kana `reading` for every kanji-bearing answer and other non-obvious readings.
- Continued mora timing, long/short vowels, small `っ`, moraic `ん`, contracted
  sounds, contextual `は/へ/を`, high-vowel devoicing, phrase boundaries, and
  standard Tokyo pitch movement.
- Counter, date, clock-time, and irregular verb readings receive explicit support.
- No rōmaji answers, arbitrary all-kana fallbacks, isolated kanji readings,
  pitch numbers, or pitch-only contrasts.

## 6. Register

- `です/ます` is the default productive register with strangers, staff, and teachers.
- Plain forms occur only in slices with an explicit friend or family relationship.
- Concise `register` support marks wording whose appropriateness changes by relationship.
- No random long/short-form mixing, broad keigo, role speech, dialect, or slang lists.

## 7. Pragmatics

- Omit recoverable subjects and objects rather than repeating pronouns.
- Keep Japanese information order and topic continuity instead of English word order.
- Distinguish acknowledgement, agreement, refusal, uncertainty, and correction.
- Keep requests soft, invitations non-presumptive, refusals tactful, and service
  formulas conventional.
- Use `ね` for shared alignment and `よ` for useful new information in natural contexts.

## 8. Listening targets

- Recover the exact hidden word, collocation, form, or compact construction from audio.
- Distinguish particles, tense, polarity, adjective class, and polite/plain endings.
- Hear counter, date, time, long-vowel, small-`っ`, moraic-`ん`, and contracted forms.
- Track omitted arguments, phrase boundaries, ongoing/resultant states, quotation,
  and reasons in short beginner sentences.

## 9. Production targets

- Type canonical Japanese orthography through an IME, including learned kanji.
- Produce one primary lexical, collocational, inflectional, or functional target per card.
- Select particles and forms from a complete situation rather than English glosses.
- Maintain polite baseline and choose plain forms only in stated close relationships.
- Produce readings, counters, dates, times, and okurigana accurately.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Complete Japanese 00 or equivalent kana, input, sound, and survival-chunk control.
- Ability to listen to a complete short utterance and type a focused hidden span.
- No productive kanji, conjugation terminology, or prior JLPT preparation is assumed.

## 11. Exclusions

- Complete Genki II or intermediate grammar.
- Productive passive, causative, causative-passive, broad conditionals, systematic
  giving/receiving, transitive/intransitive pairs, hearsay, appearance, embedded
  questions, honorific/humble systems, and intermediate nominalization or stance.
- Formal workplace writing, obscure JLPT vocabulary, isolated kanji readings,
  handwriting grading, pronunciation scoring, ASR, forced alignment, and pitch numbers.
- Anime, archaic, literary, dialectal, theatrical, or strongly gendered production.

## Kanji inventory

The following 186 high-value characters are introduced through useful words and
sentences. They are not isolated targets. A character may recur when the word is
independently useful. Slice entries below assign the practical word families.

- Identity, education, and work: `人 日 本 学 生 先 何 名 国 語 友 私 大 校 会 社 員 仕 事`.
- Family and people: `父 母 兄 姉 弟 妹 家 族 子 男 女 方 年 夫 妻 客`.
- Time and calendar: `時 分 半 午 前 後 今 毎 週 月 火 水 木 金 土 曜 朝 昼 夜 春 夏 秋 冬 間`.
- Numbers and money: `一 二 三 四 五 六 七 八 九 十 百 千 円`.
- Place, movement, and travel: `行 来 帰 駅 電 車 道 右 左 東 西 南 北 外 上 下 近 遠 入 出 乗 降 歩 旅`.
- Daily actions: `起 寝 食 飲 見 聞 読 書 話 言 買 作 使 持 待 休 働 勉 強 洗 開 閉 立 座`.
- Description and nature: `高 安 新 古 長 短 白 黒 赤 青 好 元 気 天 雨 雪 山 川 海 花 寒`.
- Body and health: `手 足 目 耳 口 体 頭 病 院 薬 熱 痛 声 助`.
- Food, shopping, and service: `店 物 肉 魚 茶 酒 料 理 服 色 注 文`.
- Thought, learning, and explanation: `思 知 教 習 住 所 有 無 同 多 少 早 遅 明 暗 楽 困 必 要`.

These 186 characters are the complete kanji inventory for Japanese 01.
Card `cloze` and `answer` values must not introduce a character outside this set.
When a lexical inventory label conventionally uses an unassigned character, use
normal beginner kana for the whole word rather than an unnatural mixed spelling.
Visible context should also avoid unsupported kanji unless it is a proper sign or
name needed for the situation. This keeps recognition breadth within 150–200
without sacrificing ordinary kana, katakana, okurigana, or useful vocabulary.

## Slice acceptance workflow

For every slice, use a new no-context generator and a different new no-context
reviewer. Provide the complete issue, only the current slice, the exact range,
and the cumulative accepted-primary-target index containing Japanese 00 and all
accepted Japanese 01 slices. After review, assemble the cumulative source,
validate it, check exact target duplication, and generate real audio for the new
slice before starting the next generator. Representative checks must include
kanji readings, counters/dates when applicable, long vowels, small `っ`, moraic
`ん`, contracted sounds, and complete polite or plain sentences.

## Acceptance

The stage is complete only when all twenty-five slices satisfy their assigned
grammar, lexical, kanji, register, listening, and production targets; the 1,000
cards and all local MP3s pass validation; Japanese 00 and 01 both pass source
checks; and the rebuilt archive reopens with both stages in lexical order and
verified media counts and hashes.
