# Japanese 04 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose and completion rule

Japanese 04 builds an original N2/B2 upper-intermediate stage in contemporary
standard Tokyo Japanese. Japanese 00–03 are binding prerequisites. Their accepted
sources contain exactly **4,300 cards and 4,300 distinct focused cloze targets**.
An audit of every accepted `sentence` in those sources finds exactly **864 distinct
kanji**. These accepted sources, rather than declarations copied from an older
matrix, are the prior authority for this stage.

This matrix specifies exactly **2,400 original cards** in sixty 40-card slices,
`ja-04-0001` through `ja-04-2400`. Its 2,400 focused productive targets bring the
cumulative accepted-primary-target count to **6,700** after all slices pass fresh
generation and review. The normalized lemma-or-useful-chunk audit contains **9,238
distinct entries** across stages 00–04. Card count is not a lexical count: this
deduplicated authority is separate from the accepted-primary-target count.
The lower planning bound is justified by the explicit target ledger and complete
trace maps below; it is not permission to stop with a missing relation, register,
domain, or final-review area, and it must never be padded toward 3,200.

The prerequisite graph is linear, cumulative, and binding. Slice 1 may use only
accepted stages 00–03. Slice *n* may use accepted stages 00–03 plus slices 1 through
*n − 1*. A generator and reviewer must reject supporting language from a later
slice at the end of every batch rather than postponing the dependency audit.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Advanced concession and clause linkage with `ものの`, `にもかかわらず`,
  `ながらも`, `とはいえ`, `にしては`, `わりに`, `どころか`, `ばかりか`,
  change `一方だ`, contrast/parallel `一方で`, `反面`, `に対して`,
  `に比べて`, `から見ると/から見れば`, `という点で`, `に応じて`, and
  `に伴って`, with distinct discourse licensing rather than translation matching.
- Source and stance contrasts among direct quotation, indirect `という`,
  `によると/によれば`, `とのことだ`, institutional `とされている`, analytical
  `と考えられる`, observational `と見られる`, attributed alternatives
  `という見方がある`, evidential `ことから`, and recommendation `を踏まえて`.
- Modality and qualification with `に違いない`, `はずだ`, `でしょう/だろう`,
  `かもしれない`, `とは限らない`, `ないことはない`, `ないわけではない`,
  `ざるを得ない`, `わけにはいかない`, `にすぎない`, `だけだ`,
  `どころではない`, `かねない`, `おそれがある`, `ことは確かだ`, and
  `可能性が高い/低い`, including force, negation scope, and realistic risk.
- Formal but readable reference with `こと`, `もの`, `点`, `面`, `結果`,
  `条件`, `上で/上では`, `際に/際は`, `にあたって`, `において`,
  `について`, `に関して`, `に対して`, compact noun modification, recoverable
  heads, and short cohesive paragraphs.
- Clear claims, reasons, evidence, examples, acknowledged limits,
  counterarguments and responses, partial agreement, qualified disagreement,
  comparison, conditional recommendation, proportional conclusion, audience
  reformulation, and source-faithful summary.
- Practical respectful and humble Japanese through common special verbs,
  `お/ご〜になる`, `お/ご〜する`, `〜てくださる`, and `〜ていただく`, always
  governed by role, action direction, and in-group/out-group perspective.
- Purpose-first formal email; short reports, incident explanations, proposals,
  result summaries, reviews, interviews, telephone turns, service recovery,
  multi-speaker discussion, interruption, resumption, reductions, ellipsis,
  backchannels, and spoken/written reformulation.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Understand, summarize, and discuss one substantial essay, interview, review, or
  explanatory source; compare two short sources without collapsing their authors.
- Present an abstract position with reasons and evidence, acknowledge a real
  limitation, answer a counterpoint, recommend under stated conditions, and stop
  at a conclusion proportional to the evidence.
- Distinguish direct fact, named source, relayed report, institutional position,
  speaker inference, evaluation, prediction, risk, limitation, and uncertainty.
- Write focused spans belonging to email requests, scheduling, confirmation,
  apology, reminder, follow-up, progress reports, incident explanations,
  proposals, recommendations, result summaries, and reviews.
- Manage visitors, ordinary workplace contact, telephone calls, messages,
  service complaints, apologies, requests, explanations of feasibility, and
  follow-up with practical rather than ornamental keigo.
- Enter and sustain discussion, handle interruption, resume an earlier point,
  clarify under time pressure, use backchannels without implying agreement,
  disagree tactfully, leave a soft unfinished turn, and reformulate for another
  audience or mode.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- The audited prior authority is exactly **864 sentence kanji** in accepted
  `cards/ja-JP/00.json` through `03.json`. The accepted Japanese 04 source adds
  **487 distinct kanji**, yielding **1,351 cumulative sentence kanji**. This count
  excludes the iteration mark `々` and ideographic zero `〇`.
- The final inventory is derived from first occurrence in accepted sentences after
  all language reviews, with zero overlap against the prior 864. Characters occur
  in useful vocabulary and coherent contexts rather than isolated reading drills.
- Use contemporary normal kanji/kana/katakana. Every kanji-bearing cloze receives
  an accurate kana `reading`; non-obvious compounds, dates, names, and counters
  receive support when material. Rōmaji, blanket kana, homophones, and approximate
  paraphrases are not automatic alternatives.
- Reinforce mora timing, vowel length, small `っ`, moraic `ん`, contracted sounds,
  compound readings, subordinate-clause boundaries, qualification focus,
  read-aloud written style, natural conversation, telephone/service intonation,
  and standard Tokyo pitch movement without accent numbers or scoring.

## 6. Register

Ordinary `です/ます` is the productive baseline with unfamiliar adults and
neutral professional contacts. Practical respect and humility require a concrete
role and correct action direction; the speaker's own action or in-group is not
honorified merely because the setting is formal. Formal email and neutral written
prose use motivated `です/ます` or concise `だ/である`. Ordinary casual forms
require an explicit friend, family member, or close peer. Telephone and service
language remains functional, not an executive-keigo conversion table.

## 7. Pragmatics

Every source and degree of commitment is explicit. Strong inference and warning
are used only with adequate grounds; limits and double negatives have audible
scope. Concessions acknowledge a real point rather than mechanically delaying a
rejection. Recommendations state their conditions. Disagreement, correction,
refusal, complaint, apology, gratitude, and service recovery preserve both role
and face. Subjects, objects, and repeated heads are omitted when recoverable;
English-style repeated `私/あなた` and translated clause chains are rejected.

## 8. Listening targets

- Recover one exact focused span from a complete utterance, never a hidden email,
  report, paragraph, or long clause chain.
- Track clause linkage, comparison basis, concession scope, source, attribution,
  institutional versus speaker stance, modality, risk, limitation, negation,
  nominal reference, role direction, in-group/out-group perspective, and focus.
- Follow interviews and multi-speaker contexts with interruption, resumption,
  reductions, ellipsis, backchannels, unfinished soft turns, and explicit cues for
  questions, names, dates, numbers, corrections, telephone, and service language.
- Distinguish neutral written read-aloud from natural spoken reformulation without
  depending on performance, dialect, or undocumented rising intonation.

## 9. Production targets

- Type one focused canonical span whose `answer` equals its single `cloze`; keep
  `accepted_answers` empty unless a genuine alternative exists.
- Produce the licensed connector, source marker, stance, risk level, nominal
  reference, practical keigo choice, discourse chunk, collocation, or written-mode
  phrase from complete visible and audible context.
- Preserve source viewpoint, certainty, role, relationship, information order,
  ellipsis, and socially plausible force. Include optional fields only when
  material and never emit empty strings.
- Reject exact, semantic, and mechanically varied duplicates before acceptance.

## 10. Prerequisites

Every slice requires the complete accepted Japanese 00–03 system: kana/IME and
sound; particles and information structure; polite and relationship-bound plain
forms; all productive inflection; relative clauses; potential, passive,
causative, and limited causative-passive; conditionals; giving/receiving;
transitivity and aspect; purpose, cause, sequence, simultaneity, concession;
appearance, report, inference, probability, `はず/わけ/べき`; nominalization;
change and action phase; introductory written style and keigo; quotation,
summary, comparison, qualified opinion, repair, ellipsis, and reductions; all
4,300 accepted primary targets; and all 864 actual prior sentence kanji.

Within Japanese 04 the graph is strictly cumulative: slice 1 has no Japanese 04
prerequisite, slice 2 requires slice 1, and slice 60 requires slices 1–59.

## 11. Exclusions

Reserve for Japanese 05 or later: the complete N1/C1 range; dense academic papers,
legal drafting, policy briefs, institutional regulations, long multi-source
synthesis, advanced rhetorical manipulation, literary style, specialized
professional jargon, obscure JLPT vocabulary, near-native humor and irony, and
systematic literary, archaic, dialectal, anime-role, theatrical, aggressive, or
strongly gendered production. Also exclude isolated readings, handwriting, pitch
numbers, voice comparison, ASR, forced alignment, pronunciation scoring, long
dictation targets, copied/translated sentences, weak synonyms, mechanical
variants, and name/number/date/tense/counter/noun-substitution padding.
