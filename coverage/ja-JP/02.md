# Japanese 02 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose and completion rule

Japanese 02 completes the fresh-context N4/A2 elementary stage in contemporary
standard Tokyo Japanese. Japanese 00 supplies kana, sound, input, and survival
chunks; Japanese 01 supplies the complete N5/A1 system, 1,000 accepted primary
targets, and exactly 186 kanji. This matrix plans exactly 1,200 original cards in
thirty independent 40-card slices, `ja-02-001` through `ja-02-1200`.

The 1,200-card lower bound is sufficient because the plan gives every required
construction a productive target and contrast practice, distributes all twelve
communicative domains, introduces 200 useful non-01 kanji through ordinary words,
and assigns forty distinct targets to every slice. It is not a reason to stop
early: completion also requires every inventory row and high-risk contrast below.
The count contains no name, number, date, counter, tense, or noun-substitution
padding. A future card author must maintain a cumulative accepted-primary-target
index for stages 00–02 and reject duplicates.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Present/past and affirmative/negative relative clauses; verb, `い`-adjective,
  `な`-adjective, and noun modification; natural omitted heads and longer NPs.
- Potential forms (including `できる` and `来られる`), natural `が/を`,
  situational possibility, `ことができる`, `〜やすい`, and `〜にくい`.
- Volitional forms; `〜と思っている`; `つもり`, `予定`, `ことにする`,
  introductory `ことになる`; `〜ておく`; `〜ようになる/ようにする`;
  `〜始める/続ける/終わる/やめる`.
- Conditionals `と/たら/ば/なら`, including their restrictions; concession
  `〜ても/〜のに`; practical regret/advice `〜ばよかった` where justified.
- Giving/receiving `あげる/くれる/もらう` and `〜てあげる/くれる/もらう`,
  viewpoint, gratitude, and softened favor requests.
- Productive common transitive/intransitive pairs; event versus agent action;
  ongoing/resultant `〜ている`; prepared `〜てある`; `〜てしまう`.
- Direct and affected-person passive; causative permission/requirement;
  contextual recognition and limited production of common causative-passives.
- Appearance `〜そうだ` versus hearsay `〜そうだ`; inference/resemblance
  `ようだ/みたいだ`; uncertainty `かもしれない/でしょう`.
- Embedded question-word `〜か` and polar `〜かどうか`; nominalizers `の/こと`
  in high-frequency `のが/のは/のを` and established patterns.
- `前に/後で/〜てから`, `間/間に`, `〜ながら`, `〜てみる`, duration,
  frequency, deadlines, sequence, habit, state, preparation, and completion.
- `〜なければならない`, spoken `〜なきゃ`, `〜なくてはいけない`,
  `〜なくてもいい`, `〜た/ないほうがいい`, `〜たらどうですか`, and
  softened requests in rules, home, school, work, and health contexts.
- Introductory subject honorification `お/ご〜になる`, humble `お/ご〜する`,
  and the listed common special verbs, always paired with an ordinary polite choice.
- Connected conversation: follow-up, confirmation, clarification, refusal,
  explanation, apology, backchannels, `ね/よ/かな`, ellipsis, ordinary reductions,
  and relationship-controlled polite/casual switching.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Describe and identify people, objects, places, experiences, plans, needs, and
  preferences through connected noun phrases.
- Explain ability, access, usability, ease, difficulty, intention, preparation,
  decisions, schedules, habit change, progress, and completion.
- State automatic, completed, hypothetical, and context-based conditions; advise,
  concede, predict consequences, and express limited regret.
- Ask for, provide, receive, decline, and acknowledge help with correct viewpoint.
- Report events and resulting/prepared states; explain accidents and repairs.
- Describe being affected, give permission, impose requirements, and recognize
  common imposed-action descriptions without overusing passive or causative.
- Distinguish appearance, reported information, inference, resemblance,
  prediction, and uncertainty using an explicit source or evidence context.
- Relay questions, check whether something is true, nominalize ordinary actions,
  sequence or overlap events, and maintain a short connected narrative.
- Negotiate obligations, permission, advice, requests, rules, and exceptions.
- interact with staff, teachers, guests, customers, and seniors using only the
  introductory honorific/humble forms motivated by those roles.
- Confirm, repair, refuse tactfully, apologize, react, and switch register only
  when a stated relationship licenses it.

## 3. Core lexical domains

The incremental inventory is the 1,200 productive targets in the slice lists,
not 1,200 dictionary headwords. It advances the cumulative accepted-primary-target
index from Japanese 00's 300 targets and Japanese 01's 1,000 targets to 2,500
targets/chunks before deduplication by lemma; supporting sentence vocabulary is
counted only after it occurs naturally in an accepted card. Domains are home
maintenance and moving (slices 1, 8, 9); school, study, exams, clubs, and teachers
(2, 12, 24); work schedules, tasks, colleagues, and absence (3, 13, 24); health,
appointments, medicine, and recovery (4, 21, 25); shopping, returns, delivery,
banking, and post (5, 16, 28); transport and travel problems (6, 17, 27); hotels,
reservations, sightseeing, and lost property (7, 18); friendships, gifts, favors,
disagreement, gratitude, and apology (10, 11, 26); hobbies, sport, media, events,
and recommendations (14, 19); weather, disasters, rules, and emergencies (15, 20);
plans, goals, habits, and change (22, 23); and news, notices, instructions,
messages, and connected narratives (27–30).

## 4. Collocations and chunks

Required families include `修理を頼む`, `故障の原因`, `荷物を運ぶ`,
`試験を受ける`, `締め切りを守る`, `欠席を連絡する`, `診察を受ける`,
`薬が効く`, `商品を交換する`, `返金を受ける`, `配達を指定する`,
`口座を開く`, `電車が遅れる`, `乗り換えを確認する`, `予約を変更する`,
`忘れ物を届ける`, `招待を断る`, `お礼を言う`, `相談に乗る`,
`記録を更新する`, `避難場所を確認する`, `目標を立てる`, and the
interactional chunks assigned below. A chunk counts only when it is a genuine
focused production target in an accepted situation; inflections and mechanical
slots do not inflate lexical breadth.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- Use normal elementary kanji/kana/katakana orthography and the exact 200-character
  addition below. Kanji are learned only through useful words and sentences.
- Every kanji-bearing cloze has an accurate kana `reading`; non-obvious compound,
  counter, or name readings also receive support. Blanket all-kana, rōmaji,
  homophone, and approximate-paraphrase answers are prohibited.
- Reinforce mora timing, long/short vowels, small `っ`, moraic `ん`, contracted
  sounds, contextual `は/へ/を`, high-vowel devoicing, compound/counter readings,
  phrase boundaries in longer clauses, Tokyo pitch movement, sentence focus,
  and careful versus ordinary polite/casual speech. No accent numbers are tested.

## 6. Register

`です/ます` is the productive baseline with strangers, staff, teachers, customers,
and unfamiliar adults. Casual forms require an explicit friend, family, or close
peer. Honorific and humble forms require a stated social role and natural reason;
each keigo slice includes ordinary polite alternatives. No random long/short
mixing, indiscriminate conversion, role speech, or needless over-politeness.

## 7. Pragmatics

Omit recoverable subjects and objects, preserve Japanese information order and
viewpoint, and avoid excessive `私/あなた`. Distinguish grammatical possibility
from situational appropriateness. Favors, beneficiary marking, refusals, gratitude,
apologies, corrections, advice, permission, requirements, and service language
must fit the relationship. In particular, unsolicited `〜てあげる` can sound
presumptuous; passive/causative and keigo are not defaults when active polite
Japanese is more natural.

## 8. Listening targets

Recover one exact focused span from a complete audio sentence; track omitted
arguments, relative-clause boundaries, particles, polarity, aspect, viewpoint,
conditional restriction, quotation/evidence source, passive/causative roles, and
register. Hear reductions such as `〜ている→〜てる`, `〜てしまう→〜ちゃう`,
and `〜なければ→〜なきゃ` for recognition only where the slice says so.
Full-sentence dictation is prohibited; whole-utterance clozes are limited to compact
fixed chunks whose complete production is itself the target.

## 9. Production targets

Type one focused word, collocation, inflected form, or compact construction in
canonical Japanese per card. The cloze occurs exactly once and equals the answer.
Keep context audible and visible enough to select viewpoint, role, evidence,
condition, aspect, and register. `accepted_answers` remains empty unless a real
alternative exists. Optional `reading`, `grammar`, `register`, or `note` fields are
included only when material and are omitted—not emitted as empty strings—otherwise.

## 10. Prerequisites

All slices require Japanese 00 and Japanese 01: kana/IME and sound control;
particles `は/が/を/に/へ/で/と/の/も/から/まで`; polite and relationship-bound
plain forms; verb/adjective/copula inflection; `て`-forms; requests, permission,
prohibition; introductory ongoing/resultant `〜ている`; quotation `と`; thought
`と思う`; invitations/proposals; `〜たい`; Japanese 01 `つもり/予定` exposure;
`たり`, experience, comparisons, reason `から`, `んです`, limited advice and
fixed `たら`, counters, time, duration, and all 186 prior kanji. Slice-specific
prerequisites appear below; a slice does not require other Japanese 02 slices
unless explicitly named.

## 11. Exclusions

Reserve Japanese 03 grammar: systematic `わけ/べき/はず`, advanced stance,
evidentiality and modality, broad abstract/written nominalization, advanced
concession/qualification, formal reports/proposals/academic argument, systematic
business keigo, and the complete Quartet I/N3 sequence. Also exclude literary,
archaic, dialectal, anime-role, strongly gendered or obscure JLPT production;
isolated readings, handwriting, pitch numbers, ASR, alignment, scoring, speculative
infrastructure, copied/translated sentences, and long translated clause chains.
