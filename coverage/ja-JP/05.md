# Japanese 05 coverage architecture

## Stage purpose and completion rule

Japanese 05 establishes an advanced C1 operating range in contemporary standard Tokyo Japanese. It develops precise clause relations, calibrated modality, source synthesis, advanced written organization, argument and mediation, audience-sensitive reformulation, professional interaction, cultural interpretation, and sustained multi-speaker communication.

The stage contains exactly **3,000 cards** in **75 slices of 40 cards**, with IDs `ja-05-0001` through `ja-05-3000`. Japanese 00–04 are binding prerequisites.

Completion requires all of the following:

- All 75 slices and all 3,000 sequential IDs are present.
- Every construction, contrast, register, communicative function, lexical domain, listening target, and production target specified below receives substantive coverage.
- Each slice preserves the exact earlier Japanese 05 dependencies stated in the architecture table.
- The 3,000 new primary targets are distinct from the accepted 6,700-target baseline and from one another.
- Lexical breadth is measured independently through the normalized lemma-or-useful-chunk authority: 3,117 accepted Japanese 05 additions bring the audited 9,281-entry baseline to 12,398 entries.
- The accepted source adds 144 sentence kanji to the audited prior 1,345-character inventory, yielding 1,489 cumulative characters through useful contemporary vocabulary.
- Formality, source ownership, certainty, implication, interpersonal force, and audience adaptation remain contextually justified.
- Numerical completion alone does not compensate for a missing inventory area or a padded distinction.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Advanced qualification and concession:
  `ないまでも`, `ないにしても`, `とはいうものの`, and their contrasts with
  `ものの`, `とはいえ`, `にしても`, `としても`, `にもかかわらず`, and
  `ながらも`.
- Commitment and binding grounds:
  `からには`, `以上（は）`, and `上は`, distinguished by volitional commitment,
  logical consequence, institutional premise, and written force.
- Endpoint and culmination:
  `に至る`, `に至って`, `に至るまで`, `に至っても`, and
  `に至った経緯`, with result, late-stage realization, escalation, and temporal
  culmination kept distinct.
- Correlated change and adaptation:
  `に伴い/に伴って`, `に応じ/に応じて`, `に即して`, `に沿って`,
  including automatic correlation, proportional response, factual conformity,
  policy conformity, and procedural alignment.
- Span, channel, and sustained means:
  `を通じて/を通して`, including temporal span, intermediary, experience,
  institutional channel, and means.
- Topic, dispute, and surrounding activity:
  `をめぐって/をめぐる`, distinguished from neutral `について`,
  formal `に関して`, response target `に対して`, and broad concern
  `にかかわる`.
- Advanced comparison:
  `にひきかえ`, `にもまして`, and contrasts with `に比べて`, `に対して`,
  `一方で`, `反面`, and `どころか`.
- Relevance and invariance:
  `にかかわる`, `にかかわらず`, `を問わず`, `によらず`, and
  `いかんにかかわらず`, with concern, consequence, independence, and
  institutional applicability distinguished.
- Imposed necessity:
  active `を余儀なくする` and affected `を余儀なくされる`, contrasted with
  `ざるを得ない`, `わけにはいかない`, and ordinary obligation.
- Unavoidable consequence and conduct:
  `ないでは済まない/ずには済まない`, distinguished from
  `ないわけにはいかない`, `なければならない`, and emotional compulsion.
- Involuntary response:
  `ずにはいられない/ないではいられない`, contrasted with
  unavoidable practical consequence and simple inability to refrain.
- Advisability and unnecessary action:
  `に越したことはない`, `までもない`, `に及ばない`, and contrasts with
  `ほうがいい`, `べきだ`, `必要はない`, and `なくてもよい`.
- Worthiness and endurance:
  `に足る`, `に足りる`, `に足りない`, `に堪える`, `に堪えない`,
  `見るに堪えない`, and `感謝に堪えない`, with evaluative and affective
  readings separated.
- Institutional specification and detached assessment:
  `ものとする`, `ものとみなす`, `ものとみられる`, `ものと思われる`,
  and `こととする`.
- Standing institutional arrangement:
  advanced `ことになっている`, contrasted with eventive `ことになった`,
  personal `ことにしている`, formal `ものとする`, custom, rule, design, and
  scheduled arrangement.
- Calibrated residual claims:
  `ことは否定できない`, `可能性を排除できない`, `とは断定できない`,
  `と言い切ることはできない`, `疑いを排除できない`, and
  `一概には言えない`.
- Source synthesis and reported stance:
  direct quotation, partial quotation, indirect report, `という`,
  `とのことだ`, `によると/によれば`, `とされている`,
  `と考えられている`, `とみられている`, `との見方を示す`,
  `との認識を示す`, `と指摘する`, `と主張する`, `と認める`,
  `と否定する`, `と反論する`, `と示唆する`, `と報じる`,
  `ことを明らかにする`, `ことが判明する`, and source-neutral synthesis.
- Evidential and epistemic distinctions:
  observation, testimony, record, official position, analytical inference,
  reported consensus, disputed interpretation, provisional conclusion,
  corroboration, contradiction, omission, and unresolved uncertainty.
- Advanced nominalization:
  `こと`, `もの`, `ところ`, `わけ`, `次第`, `旨`, `由`,
  `趣旨`, `限り`, `上`, `点`, `面`, `結果`, `経緯`, `背景`,
  `要因`, `見通し`, `方針`, `見解`, `認識`, and `立場`.
- Written organization:
  compact noun modification, apposition, recoverable heads, lexical reference,
  anaphoric `これ/それ/その点/このこと`, cataphoric framing, paragraph topics,
  enumeration, contrast, qualification, exemplification, restatement,
  consequence, reservation, transition, and conclusion.
- Argument and mediation:
  claim, grounds, warrant, evidence, example, scope, definition, assumption,
  concession, counterargument, rebuttal, qualification, trade-off,
  recommendation, reservation, compromise, consensus, dissent, and proportional
  conclusion.
- Audience reformulation:
  technical-to-general, institutional-to-public, written-to-spoken,
  expert-to-colleague, adult-to-child, service-to-client, and formal-to-neutral
  restatement without changing source commitment.
- Interactional grammar:
  turn entry, floor holding, interruption, resumption, overlap repair,
  reference repair, self-correction, other-correction, reformulation,
  confirmation, challenge, deferral, conditional acceptance, and question handling.

## 2. Communicative functions

- State a qualified advanced position and define its intended scope.
- Explain causal, concessive, comparative, proportional, and culminating relations.
- Distinguish commitment based on personal resolve, evidence, rules, and institutional premises.
- Summarize several sources while preserving ownership, chronology, certainty, and disagreement.
- Compare sources by claim, method, population, time, terminology, and evidential strength.
- Identify corroboration, contradiction, missing evidence, and unresolved questions.
- Attribute claims without converting allegations, estimates, or interpretations into facts.
- Organize reports, analytical explanations, proposals, minutes, notices, and correspondence.
- Build an argument from evidence and answer the strongest relevant counterargument.
- Mediate between competing priorities and formulate conditional areas of agreement.
- Reformulate content for audiences with different expertise, authority, age, or practical needs.
- Chair and participate in meetings, negotiate constraints, assign actions, and record reservations.
- Make tactful requests, refusals, corrections, apologies, reminders, and escalations.
- Handle professional calls, appointments, complaints, delays, exceptions, and service recovery.
- Interpret implication, understatement, indirect criticism, rhetorical reservation, and cultural framing.
- Sustain multi-speaker interaction, repair misunderstandings, present formally, and answer questions.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- The binding prior authority is the audited **1,345 distinct sentence kanji** in accepted Japanese 00–04 material. The count excludes `々` and `〇`.
- Japanese 05 progresses naturally toward the **2,136-character common-use kanji set** through frequent contemporary words, productive compounds, institutional language, media, culture, and public life.
- Priority goes to useful missing common-use characters and to strengthening weakly represented characters in additional ordinary compounds.
- No fixed number of new characters is achieved by obscure names, rare compounds, isolated readings, or literary vocabulary.
- Normal modern orthography governs kanji–kana choice, okurigana, katakana loans, abbreviations, numerals, quotation marks, parentheses, headings, and lists.
- Readings distinguish productive compound patterns, rendaku, sound changes, long vowels, moraic consonants, names, dates, counters, and formal compounds.
- Spoken work reinforces subordinate-clause boundaries, contrastive focus, negation scope, parenthetical attribution, list prosody, quotation boundaries, hesitation, repair, telephone clarity, and formal presentation delivery.
- Standard Tokyo pitch movement remains an awareness target without accent-number memorization or pitch-only distinctions.

## 6. Register

- Ordinary `です/ます` remains the default for unfamiliar adults and neutral professional interaction.
- Neutral `だ/である` supports reports, analytical prose, explanatory articles, minutes, and formal presentations when genre and audience justify it.
- Practical respectful and humble language is selected by role, action direction, benefit, and in-group/out-group perspective.
- Institutional language may use impersonal, nominal, passive, and rule-like constructions without becoming legalistic.
- Public-facing language favors clarity over bureaucratic density.
- Casual language requires an explicit close relationship and may include ellipsis, reductions, unfinished turns, and informal stance markers.
- Media language distinguishes headlines, narration, quoted speech, commentary, and attributed claims.
- Cultural criticism permits concise evaluative prose, implication, and understatement while avoiding affected literary imitation.
- Register shifts must preserve proposition, source, certainty, and interpersonal force.
- Ceremonial keigo, archaic forms, dialect performance, character speech, aggressive slang, and strongly gendered persona language are outside the productive core.

## 7. Pragmatics

- A concession must acknowledge a genuine point; it cannot serve as an empty preface.
- Strong conclusions require proportionate evidence and an explicit source or reasoning path.
- Institutional passives must not conceal a relevant responsible party when accountability matters.
- `ものとする` and similar forms require a real rule, assumption, or stipulated interpretation.
- Indirect criticism must remain recoverable without becoming needlessly vague or humiliating.
- Understatement, hesitation, and unfinished turns are interpreted through relationship, stakes, and context.
- Backchannels indicate attention or understanding, not automatic agreement.
- Refusals and corrections combine a clear practical outcome with an appropriate explanation or alternative.
- Mediation represents each party’s priorities fairly and does not manufacture consensus.
- Audience reformulation simplifies wording and background assumptions without distorting facts or certainty.
- Professional keigo avoids elevating the speaker’s own in-group and avoids unnecessary elaboration.
- Repetition of explicit subjects, pronouns, and nominal heads is avoided when reference remains clear.
- Ambiguity is repaired when it affects responsibility, deadlines, quantities, evidence, or consent.

## 8. Listening targets

- Track long sentences containing embedded attribution, parenthetical qualification, concession, and delayed predicates.
- Distinguish `ないまでも`, `ないにしても`, `とはいうものの`, and related concession patterns by scope and force.
- Hear the difference between imposed necessity, unavoidable consequence, involuntary response, recommendation, and institutional rule.
- Recover source ownership across direct quotation, paraphrase, relay, institutional stance, inference, and commentary.
- Follow two to four short sources without merging their speakers or certainty levels.
- Identify corroboration, contradiction, correction, qualification, and withheld judgment.
- Resolve omitted subjects and referents across connected turns.
- Recognize list structure, contrastive focus, negation scope, quotation boundaries, and paragraph-like spoken organization.
- Distinguish ordinary polite speech, practical keigo, read-aloud prose, meeting language, media narration, and close-peer conversation.
- Interpret implication, understatement, indirect refusal, indirect criticism, irony-like contrast, and rhetorical reservation from sufficient context.
- Follow overlap, interruption, self-repair, other-repair, abandoned starts, resumptions, backchannels, and floor transfer.
- Extract questions, objections, assumptions, and requested commitments during formal presentations.

## 9. Production targets

- Produce precise clause relations rather than selecting connectors through loose English equivalence.
- State grounds, limits, consequences, comparison bases, affected parties, and degrees of commitment explicitly when needed.
- Attribute claims to the correct source and preserve whether they are reported, inferred, disputed, provisional, or established.
- Synthesize multiple sources into a coherent account without erasing disagreement or chronology.
- Create compact, readable nominal organization for reports, explanations, proposals, notices, and minutes.
- Present a claim with relevant evidence, a real concession, a response, and a proportionate conclusion.
- Reformulate the same content for specialists, colleagues, clients, the public, children, or service users.
- Use practical respectful, humble, ordinary polite, neutral written, and close-peer forms according to relationship and action direction.
- Negotiate constraints, propose alternatives, record reservations, confirm responsibilities, and close interactions with clear next steps.
- Express implication, understatement, tactful criticism, and culturally informed evaluation without theatricality.
- Sustain multi-speaker exchanges through entry, interruption, repair, resumption, summary, and handover.
- Deliver a structured formal presentation and answer clarification, evidence, scope, and challenge questions directly.

## 10. Prerequisites

Japanese 05 requires the complete accepted Japanese 00–04 system:

- Kana, contemporary orthography, standard Tokyo sound patterns, and Japanese input.
- Productive verb, adjective, copular, particle, aspectual, conditional, passive, causative, and giving/receiving systems.
- Clause linkage, concession, cause, purpose, sequence, comparison, change, viewpoint, and nominalization.
- Reported speech, source attribution, inference, probability, limitation, risk, and negation scope.
- Neutral written prose, summaries, reports, proposals, reviews, correspondence, and introductory institutional language.
- Respectful, humble, ordinary polite, telephone, visitor, and service interaction.
- Qualified argument, comparison, counterargument, audience reformulation, repair, ellipsis, reductions, and multi-speaker discussion.
- The accepted baseline of **6,700 primary targets**, **9,281 normalized lemma-or-useful-chunk entries**, and **1,345 distinct sentence kanji**.

Within Japanese 05, only the exact earlier slices named in the architecture table are binding Japanese 05 prerequisites.

## 11. Exclusions

- Specialist legal drafting, binding contract language, judicial analysis, and statute interpretation.
- Dense research papers requiring field-specific methods or notation.
- Specialist medical, engineering, financial, scientific, or security terminology without broad public value.
- Literary imitation, classical Japanese, systematic archaic language, and obscure idiomatic display.
- Dialect production, theatrical role speech, anime personas, aggressive slang, and strongly gendered performance.
- Ceremonial or executive keigo studied apart from a plausible role and action direction.
- Humor, sarcasm, or irony whose interpretation depends on inaccessible cultural assumptions.
- Manipulative rhetoric, deceptive euphemism, coercive negotiation, and face-threatening debate performance.
- Isolated character readings, rare-name accumulation, spelling trivia, or unnatural compounds used only to increase kanji totals.
- Mechanical paraphrase families, trivial substitutions, and distinctions without independent communicative value.

## Normalized lemma-or-useful-chunk authority

The independent prior authority contains **9,281 normalized lemma-or-useful-chunk entries** across Japanese 00–04. It merges inflectional variants and mechanically equivalent forms while retaining independently useful senses, collocations, institutional expressions, discourse markers, interactional routines, and reusable chunks.

Japanese 05 additions belong to this authority only when they contribute a new useful lemma, sense, collocation, or chunk. The final Japanese 05 lexical total must therefore be derived independently; it must not be inferred by adding 3,000 to 9,281. Repeated grammatical realizations, inflections, names, numbers, and mechanically varied substitutions do not increase this lexical authority.

The final accepted-source audit found 3,121 distinct normalized lemma-or-useful-chunk keys in Japanese 05. Four link to an existing stages 00–04 authority entry, leaving **3,117 incremental entries** and **12,398 cumulative entries**. Normalization applies Unicode compatibility normalization and merges whitespace, punctuation, inflectional, and mechanically equivalent forms; independently useful lemma and chunk senses remain separate. This approaches the lower planning guidance without misreporting inflections or mechanical substitutions as additional breadth.

## Sentence-kanji authority and common-use progression

The audited prior authority contains **1,345 distinct sentence kanji** across Japanese 00–04. Japanese 05 will:

1. Preserve this 1,345-character base through recurring useful vocabulary.
2. Prioritize missing high-frequency common-use characters needed for institutional, professional, media, cultural, civic, and analytical language.
3. Introduce characters through ordinary words and productive compounds rather than isolated readings.
4. Reinforce new characters across more than one domain where natural.
5. Prefer broadly useful modern vocabulary when several words could introduce the same character.
6. Move the cumulative inventory naturally toward the 2,136 common-use characters without treating complete character-set exhaustion as more important than contemporary usefulness.
7. Leave rare, name-dependent, literary, and specialist characters for later reading-oriented work.

The final sentence audit reconfirmed **1,345 prior characters**, found **1,279 characters used in Japanese 05**, and identified **144 incremental characters**, for **1,489 cumulative sentence kanji**. This remains below full common-use inventory coverage because the missing characters are not all licensed by frequent, authentic production in the selected advanced tasks. Adding rare names, literary compounds, or isolated readings only to raise the count would violate the stage boundary; later reading-oriented work can introduce them when ordinary source contexts justify them.
