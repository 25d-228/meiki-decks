# Japanese 00 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

Japanese 00 establishes direct kana and katakana input, the core sound system,
and neutral beginner classroom and survival language in standard Tokyo
Japanese. The planned 300 cards are divided into ten independent 30-card
slices. Completion depends on the inventory and functional coverage below,
not on filling the numerical range.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Fixed greetings, acknowledgements, requests, and service formulas.
- Polite identity patterns with `です`, including `〜です` and `〜ではありません`.
- Basic questions with `か` and question words inside memorized short patterns.
- Polite requests with `〜てください` as whole chunks, not as a conjugation lesson.
- Simple permission and prohibition chunks such as `いいですか` and `だめです`.
- Basic preference, need, possession, location, and availability chunks.
- Particles `は`, `へ`, and `を` in common phrases, with contextual pronunciation.
- A small set of time, destination, quantity, and choice frames needed for survival.

These are usable chunks and light pattern exposure. A complete N5 grammar
sequence is reserved for Japanese 01.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Greet, take leave, thank, apologize, and respond politely.
- Follow classroom instructions and ask for repetition, clarification, spelling,
  or slower speech.
- Identify oneself and give a minimal affiliation or origin.
- Confirm, deny, indicate understanding, and repair misunderstanding.
- Ask and answer basic time and scheduling questions.
- Locate facilities, ask directions, use transport, and handle lodging basics.
- Buy, pay, order food, and manage simple service encounters.
- Express a need or preference, ask permission, make a simple request, and seek help.
- Communicate basic safety and emergency needs.

## 3. Core lexical domains

- Kana-bearing everyday words chosen to cover the productive script inventory.
- Classroom objects, actions, and interaction language.
- Identity, countries, languages, roles, and basic personal information.
- Places, directions, stations, vehicles, tickets, rooms, and lodging.
- Shops, prices, money, payment, food, drinks, and counters used in fixed chunks.
- Clock time, days, meetings, openings, closings, and simple schedules.
- Health, safety, police, lost items, phones, toilets, and urgent assistance.

Broad thematic vocabulary lists and productive kanji study are excluded.

## 4. Collocations and chunks

The stage includes approximately 150 high-value communicative chunks in
slices 6–10. They must be contemporary, neutral or polite, and useful as
complete utterances. Core families include `おはようございます`,
`ありがとうございます`, `すみません`, `もういちどおねがいします`,
`ゆっくりおねがいします`, `〜はどこですか`, `〜までおねがいします`,
`これをください`, `いくらですか`, `たすけてください`, and similarly
practical formulas. Near-duplicate substitutions do not count as distinct
primary targets.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- Five stable vowels and their kana spellings.
- Mora counting and even timing in short words and phrases.
- Hiragana long-vowel patterns and katakana `ー`.
- Small `っ` and short/long consonant contrasts.
- Moraic `ん` before common consonant classes and at phrase boundaries.
- Voiced, voiceless, and semi-voiced contrasts.
- Common contracted sounds and productive small-vowel katakana combinations.
- Important consonant contrasts, including `し/ち/つ`, `ふ`, and Japanese `ら` rows.
- Introductory awareness of high-vowel devoicing in ordinary polite speech.
- Phrase boundaries, rhythm, and exposure to standard Tokyo pitch movement.

Pitch accent and devoicing remain listening-awareness targets. Typed answers
never differ only by pitch and never include accent numbers.

## 6. Register

- Contemporary standard Tokyo Japanese.
- Neutral and polite beginner language suitable for strangers, staff, and class.
- Casual forms appear only when they are unavoidable recognition targets.
- No anime-style, archaic, theatrical, strongly gendered, dialectal, or
  unnecessarily honorific production.

## 7. Pragmatics

- Use `すみません` appropriately for attention, apology, and light thanks.
- Prefer soft requests and conventional service formulas over translated commands.
- Distinguish acknowledgement from agreement where a beginner needs it.
- Use context-appropriate greetings and leave-taking.
- Avoid overusing explicit pronouns or unnatural translated word order.
- Keep emergency language direct while ordinary requests remain polite.

## 8. Listening targets

- Identify the exact hidden kana or chunk from the complete spoken utterance.
- Hear mora count, vowel length, small `っ`, moraic `ん`, voicing, and contracted sounds.
- Recognize contextual pronunciation of written `は`, `へ`, and `を`.
- Notice ordinary phrase boundaries, rhythm, polite-ending devoicing, and Tokyo pitch
  movement without being graded on accent labels.
- Distinguish common katakana loanword shapes from English spelling expectations.

## 9. Production targets

- Type canonical hiragana or katakana through a Japanese IME.
- Produce one intended word, sound contrast, spelling, or communicative chunk per card.
- Preserve small kana, `っ`, `ん`, long-vowel spelling, dakuten, handakuten, and `ー`.
- Use kana and katakana only as expected answers; rōmaji is never accepted.
- Produce complete neutral or polite utterances for the communicative slices.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Ability to enable and select a Japanese IME.
- Ability to listen to a short complete utterance and type a focused answer.
- No prior kana, kanji, grammar, or linguistics knowledge is assumed.

## 11. Exclusions

- Complete N5 grammar or broad vocabulary coverage.
- Productive kanji, rōmaji answers, and handwriting stroke grading.
- Detailed pitch-accent notation, accent-number memorization, or pitch-only answers.
- Dialect production, literary language, character-role speech, or slang lists.
- Pronunciation scoring, ASR, forced alignment, and phonetic analysis.
- Mechanical chart recitation, trivial substitutions, and isolated grammar lectures.

- [x] Common orthographic examples distinguish standard `じ/ぢ` and `ず/づ` usage.

## Fresh-context slices

### Slice 1 — `ja-00-001`–`ja-00-030`

- Primary targets: five vowels; basic `あ/か/さ/た/な`-row hiragana; short
  mora-timed words; direct hiragana IME output.
- Script and sound: `あいうえお`, `かきくけこ`, `さしすせそ`,
  `たちつてと`, `なにぬねの`; stable vowels; `し/ち/つ` awareness.
- Functions: recognize and type useful short words and very short utterances,
  not chart labels.

### Slice 2 — `ja-00-031`–`ja-00-060`

- Primary targets: remaining basic hiragana and common words using each row.
- Script and sound: `は/ま/や/ら/わ` rows, `を`, `ん`, `ふ`, Japanese
  `ら`-row, moraic `ん`, and particles `は/へ/を` in fixed phrases.
- Functions: identify objects and people, acknowledge, and use a few complete
  kana-only phrases.

### Slice 3 — `ja-00-061`–`ja-00-090`

- Primary targets: productive dakuten, handakuten, small `っ`, and common
  contracted hiragana sounds through useful words and chunks.
- Script and sound: `が/ざ/だ/ば/ぱ` rows, `じ/ぢ`, `ず/づ`,
  `きゃ/きゅ/きょ` families, voicing, gemination, and mora count.
- Functions: type familiar everyday words, confirmations, and short polite forms.

### Slice 4 — `ja-00-091`–`ja-00-120`

- Primary targets: the modern basic katakana inventory in productive loanwords.
- Script and sound: basic katakana rows, `ン`, voiced and semi-voiced forms,
  loanword mora timing, and Japanese consonant adaptation.
- Functions: recognize and type common signs, objects, food, transport, and
  technology words; `ヲ` is recognition-only.

### Slice 5 — `ja-00-121`–`ja-00-150`

- Primary targets: katakana `ー`, small `ッ`, contracted forms, productive
  foreign-sound combinations, and key length/consonant contrasts.
- Script and sound: `ティ/ディ`, `ファ/フィ/フェ/フォ`, `ウィ/ウェ/ウォ`,
  `シェ/チェ/ジェ`, long vowels, small vowels, small `ッ`, and phrase rhythm.
- Functions: type modern loanwords, names of services, and short katakana-bearing
  utterances without following English spelling.

### Slice 6 — `ja-00-151`–`ja-00-180`

- Primary targets: 30 distinct greetings, leave-taking, thanks, apologies,
  politeness, and classroom-instruction chunks.
- Script and sound: kana consolidation, polite-ending devoicing awareness,
  phrase boundaries, and natural rhythm.
- Functions: open and close interactions, respond politely, follow classroom
  directions, and attract attention.

### Slice 7 — `ja-00-181`–`ja-00-210`

- Primary targets: 30 distinct repetition, clarification, spelling, slower-speech,
  self-identification, confirmation, and time/scheduling chunks.
- Script and sound: particles `は/を`, long vowels, moraic `ん`, numbers and time
  inside fixed phrases, and IME repair.
- Functions: repair communication, identify oneself, confirm understanding,
  and arrange a basic time.

### Slice 8 — `ja-00-211`–`ja-00-240`

- Primary targets: 30 distinct location, direction, transport, ticket, and
  lodging survival chunks.
- Script and sound: particle `へ`, long vowels, small `っ`, katakana place and
  transport words, and question rhythm.
- Functions: find facilities, follow directions, board transport, buy a ticket,
  check in, and solve a basic lodging problem.

### Slice 9 — `ja-00-241`–`ja-00-270`

- Primary targets: 30 distinct shopping, payment, food, drink, and service chunks.
- Script and sound: particle `を`, counters inside fixed chunks, katakana menu
  words, contracted sounds, and polite service rhythm.
- Functions: ask a price, choose, order, pay, request an item, and handle a
  simple restaurant or shop interaction.

### Slice 10 — `ja-00-271`–`ja-00-300`

- Primary targets: 30 distinct need, preference, permission, simple-request,
  help, health, and safety chunks.
- Script and sound: cumulative long/short vowels, small `っ`, moraic `ん`,
  voicing, contractions, phrase rhythm, and Tokyo pitch-movement exposure.
- Functions: state a need or preference, ask permission, request help, report
  a basic problem, and respond in an emergency.

## Acceptance

The stage is complete when every checklist item and slice target is represented
without padding, all 300 cards pass source validation, every slice has separate
fresh generation and review, representative first-slice audio passes before
later generation, all local audio passes, and the Japanese-only archive reopens
with verified counts and hashes.
