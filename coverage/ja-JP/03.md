# Japanese 03 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose and completion rule

Japanese 03 builds an original N3/B1 independent-user stage in contemporary
standard Tokyo Japanese. Japanese 00–02 are binding prerequisites. Their accepted
sources contain exactly **2,500 cards and 2,500 distinct cloze targets**. An audit
of every accepted `sentence` in those sources finds **410 distinct kanji**, which
is the prior character authority for this matrix.

This matrix plans exactly **1,800 original cards** in forty-five 40-card slices,
`ja-03-0001` through `ja-03-1800`. Its 1,800 distinct focused productive targets
bring the cumulative accepted-target count to **4,300** when all slices are
accepted. The accepted cards introduce 454 useful characters absent from the actual
prior 410 through ordinary vocabulary, bringing the actual cumulative kanji
inventory to **864**. The lower bound is sufficient because every required family,
domain, mode, contrast, and final audit area is assigned below; no target is a
name, number, tense, counter, or noun-substitution padding item.

The prerequisite graph is cumulative and binding: slice 1 uses only stages 00–02,
and slice *n* may use stages 00–02 plus slices 1 through *n − 1*. A generator must
reject any supporting construction outside that set before accepting its slice.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Cause and grounds with contextual contrasts among `から/ので`, `ため（に）`,
  `ものだから`, `おかげで`, `せいで`, and explanatory backgrounding.
- Contrast, parallel development, and concession with `一方で`, `その一方で`,
  `反面`, `〜ても`, `〜のに`, `〜ながら（も）`, `〜くせに`, and `〜わりに`,
  each tied to its discourse relation and pragmatic force.
- Purpose, result, sequence, simultaneity, and background with `ために`, `ように`,
  `うちに`, `間/間に`, `最中に`, `たびに`, action-phase `ところ`,
  `につれて`, `にしたがって`, and readable Japanese clause chains.
- Evidence and report source contrasts among appearance/hearsay `そうだ`,
  `らしい`, evidence-based `ようだ`, conversational `みたいだ`, uncertainty
  `かもしれない`, prediction `でしょう/だろう`, and evidence-based `はず`.
- Complete productive families `わけだ`, `わけではない`, `わけがない`,
  `わけにはいかない`, `はずだ`, `はずがない`, `はずだった`, `べきだ`,
  and `べきではない`, including logical, social, evidential, and moral force.
- Event and claim reference with `の`, `こと`, `ということ`, `というの`,
  recoverable omitted heads/arguments, and topic/focus across connected turns.
- Change and aspect with `ていく/てくる`, `ようになる/ようにする`,
  `ことになる/ことにする`, `始める/続ける/終わる`, common `〜きる` and
  `〜かける`, and connected uses of `ている/てある/ておく/てしまう`.
- Ordinary sentence-final stance, explanatory `んだ/んです`, quotative `って`,
  naming/characterization `っていう`, and relationship-bound reductions
  `〜てる`, `〜ちゃう`, `〜とく`, and `〜なきゃ`.
- Polite conversation, close-peer casual conversation, informal messages, neutral
  explanatory prose, and introductory `だ/である` style with source attribution.
- Chronological retelling, attributed report, explanation, summary, comparison,
  qualified opinion, follow-up, interpretation check, correction, reformulation,
  and tactful disagreement in connected but cloze-focused production.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Sustain connected conversation about study, work, housing, health, travel,
  relationships, culture, technology, environment, news, and personal change.
- Explain causes, consequences, purposes, contrasts, concessions, expectations,
  failed expectations, uncertainty, evidence, and information sources.
- Retell events with sequence and viewpoint; report what another person said,
  believed, expected, or inferred without erasing attribution.
- Summarize a main point and supporting detail; compare alternatives or accounts;
  state a qualified opinion with evidence and socially plausible force.
- Ask focused follow-up questions, check an interpretation, correct a
  misunderstanding, reformulate an unclear point, and confirm the repair.
- Advise, judge, refuse, disagree, reserve judgment, partially agree, apologize,
  thank, and make requests with register appropriate to the relationship.
- Follow and produce ordinary ellipsis, omitted arguments, short reactions,
  unfinished soft turns, canonical forms, and the approved spoken reductions.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- Use normal intermediate kanji/kana/katakana orthography and only the prior 410
  plus the exact 454-character addition below. Kanji enter through words and
  sentences, never isolated reading cards.
- Every kanji-bearing cloze receives an accurate kana `reading`; non-obvious
  compounds also receive support. Rōmaji, blanket all-kana fallbacks, homophones,
  and approximate paraphrases are not automatically accepted.
- Reinforce mora timing, vowel length, small `っ`, moraic `ん`, contracted sounds,
  contextual `は/へ/を`, compound readings, high-vowel devoicing, phrase boundaries
  in subordinate clauses, sentence focus, and standard Tokyo pitch movement.
- Listening distinguishes careful, ordinary polite, close-peer casual, message,
  and neutral written read-aloud styles; no pitch numbers or pronunciation scoring.

## 6. Register

`です/ます` remains the productive baseline with unfamiliar adults, teachers,
staff, and professional contacts. Casual targets explicitly name a friend, family
member, or close peer. Message targets have an explicit sender/recipient; neutral
written targets name an article, notice, summary, or explanation. Introductory
`だ/である` is limited to motivated concise exposition. No systematic business
keigo, hierarchy drill, advanced correspondence, role speech, or random mixing.

## 7. Pragmatics

Evidence, report source, probability, expectation, and inference must be audible
from the complete situation. `べき` is reserved for justified norms or firm moral
judgment and is contrasted with softer advice. `くせに` is limited to warranted
close or quoted criticism. Refusals, corrections, favors, apologies, gratitude,
partial agreement, and disagreement preserve face and viewpoint. Recoverable
subjects, objects, and heads are omitted; `私/あなた` and repeated nouns are not
used to imitate English information structure.

## 8. Listening targets

- Recover one exact focused span from a complete utterance, never a long hidden
  clause chain; whole-utterance clozes are only compact interactional chunks.
- Track clause boundaries, temporal scope, contrast/concession, evidence source,
  quotation, probability, expectation, nominalizer scope, aspect, and viewpoint.
- Recognize reductions and ellipsis from relationship and context, and map approved
  reductions to canonical forms where the slice requires canonical production.
- Hear paragraph connectors, source attribution, sentence focus, backchannels,
  unfinished soft turns, and interrogative cues without undocumented rising tone.

## 9. Production targets

- Type one focused word, collocation, inflected form, or compact construction in
  canonical Japanese; `sentence` contains the cloze once and `answer == cloze`.
- Produce connected cause, evidence, stance, aspect, report, summary, comparison,
  and repair choices from complete contexts rather than English gloss matching.
- Keep `accepted_answers` empty unless a genuine alternative exists; include
  `reading`, `grammar`, `register`, or `note` only when material and omit empty fields.
- Maintain stage-approved register, orthography, natural ellipsis, question
  punctuation/endings, and fair audio identification of the hidden span.

## 10. Prerequisites

All slices require the complete accepted Japanese 00–02 system: kana/IME and sound;
particles; polite and relationship-bound plain forms; verb/adjective/copula
inflection; relative clauses; potential, passive, causative, and limited
causative-passive; conditionals; giving/receiving; transitivity; `ている/てある/
ておく/てしまう`; appearance/hearsay `そうだ`; introductory `ようだ/みたいだ`,
`かもしれない/でしょう`; ordinary `の/こと`; sequence and simultaneity;
obligation/advice; introductory keigo; repair, ellipsis, and reductions; all 2,500
accepted clozes; and all 410 actual prior sentence kanji.

Within Japanese 03 the exact graph is linear and cumulative. Slice 1 has no
Japanese 03 prerequisite. Slice 2 requires slice 1; slice 3 requires slices 1–2;
and so on through slice 45, which requires slices 1–44. This conservative graph
makes every earlier accepted target available while forbidding future-slice use.

## 11. Exclusions

Reserve for Japanese 04 or later: the full Quartet II/N2 sequence; dense essay,
report, proposal, academic, or institutional syntax; long-text source synthesis;
advanced concession, rhetorical qualification, or formal nominalization;
systematic practical/business keigo and workplace hierarchy; formal correspondence
beyond simple messages and neutral email; obscure JLPT vocabulary; literary,
archaic, dialectal, anime-role, theatrical, aggressive, or strongly gendered
production; isolated readings; handwriting; pitch numbers; ASR; forced alignment;
pronunciation scoring; and copied, translated, mechanically varied, or padded cards.
