# Mexican Spanish 01 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

- Locale and production model: `es-MX`, educated contemporary central Mexican Spanish.
- Canonical learner input: NFC Spanish with standard accents, `ñ`, inverted question and
  exclamation marks, and canonical punctuation.
- Planned size: exactly 800 original cards, `es-01-0001` through `es-01-0800`.
- Structure: twenty independent 40-card slices. Coverage and originality, not ID completion alone,
  determine whether a slice is accepted.
- Address model: singular `tú` for explicit close-peer, friend, sibling, partner, or child
  relationships; singular `usted` for unfamiliar adults, staff, and polite service; `ustedes` for
  all plural address.
- Entry point: no Spanish prerequisite beyond hearing a short cue and typing standard Latin text.
  Each later slice requires all earlier accepted slices and their primary-target index.

Every card tests one focused, normalized-unique production span in a complete natural sentence or
short coherent exchange. A required construction must occur inside the cloze itself. Whole-sentence
targets are limited to compact reusable fixed expressions. Name, noun, number, date, gender, person,
or tense substitution alone does not create new coverage.

## Binding stage-wide scope

### Grammar and constructions

- Noun gender and number; definite and indefinite articles; common adjective agreement and
  position.
- Natural subject-pronoun use and ordinary omission when the verb and discourse identify the
  subject.
- Present tense of productive regular verbs and communicatively useful forms of `ser`, `estar`,
  `hay`, `tener`, `ir`, `venir`, `hacer`, `poder`, `querer`, `deber`, `decir`, `dar`, `ver`,
  `saber`, `conocer`, `poner`, `salir`, `traer`, `llegar`, `llevar`, `parecer`, `necesitar`,
  `gustar`, `quedar`, `pasar`, and other matrix-justified A1 verbs.
- Ordinary negation; information and yes/no questions; possessive and demonstrative adjectives
  and the demonstrative pronouns needed for practical A1 reference.
- Numbers, dates, time, age, prices, quantities, telephone numbers, and basic measurements.
- `ir a + infinitive`, `tener que`, and useful `poder`, `querer`, and `deber` patterns.
- High-frequency place, movement, origin, destination, time, accompaniment, and instrument
  prepositions.
- Introductory direct-object pronouns only in short contexts with one unmistakable referent.
- Short practical links with `y`, `o`, `pero`, `porque`, `entonces`, `también`, and `tampoco`.

### Communicative functions and domains

- Greetings, introductions, thanks, apologies, clarification, confirmation, repetition, and repair.
- Identity, nationality, language, contact details, work, and study.
- Family and ordinary relationships; home, rooms, belongings, possession, existence, and location.
- Daily routine, frequency, food, drink, ordering, quantities, preferences, and payment.
- Shopping for availability, size, color, price, quantity, and ordinary goods.
- Transport, destination, direction, routes, and travel time.
- Days, dates, appointments, opening times, schedules, invitations, and concrete plans.
- Weather, seasons, simple activities, immediate needs, and non-specialist everyday problems.
- Preferences, choices, requests, routine service encounters, and polite transaction completion.

### Pronunciation, orthography, register, and originality

- Listening burdens recycle stable Spanish vowels, lexical stress, tap/trill distinctions, Mexican
  `ll/y`, Spanish `j`, `ñ`, ordinary linking, and natural phrase rhythm in meaningful contexts.
- Answers use standard spelling, never IPA, phonetic respelling, or pronunciation labels.
- Use ordinary Mexican choices such as `celular`, `computadora`, `camión` where context means city
  bus, `boleto`, `jugo`, and relationship-appropriate interaction where these are natural; avoid
  caricature and unexplained slang.
- Productive `vosotros` and `vos` are prohibited. Plural address uses `ustedes`.
- Do not create translated-English phrasing, dictionary blocks, paradigm tables, arbitrary target
  enlargement, connector-plus-subject fragments, or mechanical substitution series.

### Mexican Spanish 02 boundary

Reserve productive past narration, indirect-object pronouns, combined clitics, reflexive systems,
commands, broad `por/para`, productive `se`, progressive and other periphrases, and relative-clause
expansion for Mexican Spanish 02. Fixed lexicalized expressions may appear only when they are
indivisible, frequent A1 interactional chunks and do not teach the reserved system productively.

## Slice plan and binding burdens

### Slice 1 — `es-01-0001`–`es-01-0040`: greetings, introductions, and basic identity

- Grammar and construction burden: subject pronouns and natural omission; singular present forms of
  `ser`; `me llamo`; definite and indefinite singular articles; statement and yes/no-question form.
- Communicative function: greet, introduce oneself or another person, exchange names, thank, and
  take leave in first meetings and close-peer encounters.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: names, greetings, courtesy titles, `mucho gusto`,
  `buenos días`, `hasta luego`, and basic person nouns.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: stable vowels, stress in `días`, `cómo`, and `también`,
  initial `ll/y`, and inverted question marks.
- Register and relationship context: `usted` for unfamiliar adults; `tú` only with an explicitly
  identified friend or close peer.
- Listening and production targets: recover a greeting, identity form, article, or compact courtesy
  chunk from a complete introduction cue.
- Prerequisites: none beyond the stage entry point.
- Exclusions: nationality detail, family descriptions, plural address, and extended biography.

### Slice 2 — `es-01-0041`–`es-01-0080`: origin, nationality, language, work, and study

- Grammar and construction burden: `ser` for origin and occupation; adjective gender agreement;
  present `hablar`, `estudiar`, `trabajar`, and `vivir`; `de`, `en`, and natural subject omission.
- Communicative function: exchange origin, nationality, languages, occupation, workplace, school,
  and course information.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: countries, Mexican nationalities, languages, jobs, schools,
  offices, `hablar español`, `trabajar en`, and `estudiar en`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: written nationality endings, stress in `México`, `inglés`,
  and `español`, Spanish `j`, and silent `h`.
- Register and relationship context: neutral conversation with a new classmate, colleague,
  receptionist, or neighbor.
- Listening and production targets: distinguish identity, origin, workplace, and language spans and
  type their standard accented forms.
- Prerequisites: accepted slice 1.
- Exclusions: employment history, résumés, past study, and profession substitution tables.

### Slice 3 — `es-01-0081`–`es-01-0120`: family, relationships, age, and possession

- Grammar and construction burden: noun gender and plural number; `tener`; age with `tener`;
  possessive adjectives; common adjective agreement and position; basic `de` possession.
- Communicative function: identify family and close relationships, state ages, and describe a few
  ordinary personal qualities or possessions.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: immediate family, partner, friend, ages, `mi familia`,
  `tener ... años`, and `es de`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: `ñ` in `años`, tap/trill in relationship words, plural linking,
  and stress in possessive noun groups.
- Register and relationship context: respectful third-person description; `tú` only inside an
  explicit family or close-friend exchange.
- Listening and production targets: recover possession, age, number, or agreement from a natural
  family-information cue.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–2.
- Exclusions: object pronouns, reflexive relationships, family narratives, and mechanical gender
  pairs.

### Slice 4 — `es-01-0121`–`es-01-0160`: home, objects, existence, and location

- Grammar and construction burden: `hay` for existence; `estar` for location; articles and plurals;
  demonstrative adjectives; `en`, `sobre`, `debajo de`, `delante de`, `detrás de`, `junto a`, and
  `cerca de`.
- Communicative function: describe a home, ask where an object or room is, identify nearby places,
  and state whether something is available.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: rooms, furniture, appliances, keys, belongings, neighborhood
  places, `hay un`, `está en`, and `queda cerca`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: stress contrasts in `esta`/`está`, Spanish `j`, linking across
  location phrases, and canonical demonstrative spelling without obsolete accents.
- Register and relationship context: neutral household description and polite housing or reception
  questions.
- Listening and production targets: distinguish existence from location and recover one focused
  spatial or demonstrative span.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–3.
- Exclusions: relative clauses, home-repair commands, property contracts, and broad `se` use.

### Slice 5 — `es-01-0161`–`es-01-0200`: routine, regular present tense, and frequency

- Grammar and construction burden: useful present forms of regular `-ar`, `-er`, and `-ir` verbs;
  ordinary subject omission; `no`; frequency expressions; short links with `y`, `pero`, and `porque`.
- Communicative function: state daily and weekly routines, recurring work or study actions, leisure,
  frequency, and simple reasons.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: weekdays, meals, work, classes, reading, walking, music,
  `todos los días`, `a veces`, and `casi nunca`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: stress that distinguishes present forms, tap in verb endings,
  linking, and phrase-final statement contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral self-description and relationship-supported close-peer
  questions.
- Listening and production targets: identify a regular present form, negation, connector, or
  frequency chunk from connected delivery.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–4.
- Exclusions: productive reflexive routines, progressive forms, past narration, and conjugation
  tables.

### Slice 6 — `es-01-0201`–`es-01-0240`: movement and essential irregular present forms

- Grammar and construction burden: useful present forms of `ir`, `venir`, `salir`, `llegar`,
  `llevar`, `traer`, and `hacer`; destinations with `a`; origins with `de`; accompaniment with
  `con`.
- Communicative function: say where someone goes or comes from, when they leave or arrive, what they
  bring or carry, and what ordinary activity they do.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: home, work, school, market, station, appointments,
  `ir al trabajo`, `venir de`, `salir de casa`, and `llegar a tiempo`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: `ll/y` in `llego` and `llevo`, Spanish `j` in `trabajo`, tap in
  `traer`, and connected prepositional phrases.
- Register and relationship context: neutral daily coordination with relatives, peers, coworkers,
  or polite contacts.
- Listening and production targets: recover one irregular form or movement collocation directly
  supported by origin, destination, time, or object context.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–5.
- Exclusions: motion narratives, commands, reflexive departure verbs, and person-by-person paradigms.

### Slice 7 — `es-01-0241`–`es-01-0280`: ability, wants, obligation, and immediate needs

- Grammar and construction burden: `poder`, `querer`, `deber`, `necesitar`, `tener que`, and an
  infinitive; ordinary negation; polite yes/no questions without command production.
- Communicative function: express ability, permission, desire, preference, obligation, and a clear
  immediate need; accept or decline a feasible action.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: schedules, help, calls, forms, rest, food, transport,
  `puedo + infinitivo`, `quiero + sustantivo`, and `tengo que + infinitivo`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: vowel changes in modal forms, stress in infinitive groups,
  tap/trill distinctions, and question contour.
- Register and relationship context: direct but courteous service questions; neutral or close-peer
  statements elsewhere.
- Listening and production targets: distinguish ability, desire, need, and obligation and type one
  focused modal span.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–6.
- Exclusions: conditional politeness, commands, moral advice, and mechanical modal substitution.

### Slice 8 — `es-01-0281`–`es-01-0320`: information questions, clarification, and repair

- Grammar and construction burden: `qué`, `quién`, `cuál`, `dónde`, `cuándo`, `cómo`, `cuánto`, and
  `por qué`; present `decir`, `saber`, `conocer`, and `ver`; confirmation and ordinary negation.
- Communicative function: ask for a missing detail, request repetition or spelling, confirm what was
  heard, correct a basic misunderstanding, and say whether one knows a fact, person, or place.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: names, words, addresses, signs, people, places,
  `¿Cómo se dice...?` as a fixed repair chunk, `no sé`, and `¿Conoce...?`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: written accents on question words, inverted punctuation,
  distinction between statement and question contour, and natural question rhythm.
- Register and relationship context: `usted` in service or unfamiliar interaction; explicit `tú`
  only with a close peer.
- Listening and production targets: recover the question word, knowledge verb, confirmation span,
  or compact repair expression that resolves the stated uncertainty.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–7.
- Exclusions: embedded questions, indirect speech, broad `se`, and translation drills.

### Slice 9 — `es-01-0321`–`es-01-0360`: numbers and personal contact details

- Grammar and construction burden: cardinal numbers, age review, telephone numbers, addresses,
  floors, room numbers, prices, quantities, `cuánto`, and singular/plural agreement.
- Communicative function: give and verify a phone number, address, room, quantity, price, or simple
  measurement needed in a practical exchange.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: `celular`, street and apartment details, pesos, pieces,
  liters, kilos, `¿Cuánto cuesta?`, and grouped digit strings.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: number stress and linking, `c`/`s` realization in Mexican
  Spanish, clear digit grouping, and standard written number words where targeted.
- Register and relationship context: polite exchange of necessary personal or transaction details;
  no unnecessary private information.
- Listening and production targets: distinguish a complete practical number span from surrounding
  units or labels without relying on a number-only cue.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–8.
- Exclusions: arithmetic drills, dense account numbers, mechanical price substitution, and ordinal
  systems beyond immediate A1 use.

### Slice 10 — `es-01-0361`–`es-01-0400`: dates, time, schedules, and concrete plans

- Grammar and construction burden: weekdays, months, dates, clock time, `a`, `de ... a`, `antes de`,
  `después de`; opening times; `ir a + infinitive`; present `quedar` for arrangements.
- Communicative function: ask and state time or date, arrange an appointment, check hours, describe
  a schedule, and state a near-term concrete plan.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: calendars, classes, clinics, stores, meetings,
  `tener una cita`, `quedar a las`, `abre a`, and `voy a + infinitivo`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: stress in day and month names, linking in clock expressions,
  question contour, and canonical date punctuation.
- Register and relationship context: polite scheduling with staff or a new contact; explicit
  close-peer planning with `tú`.
- Listening and production targets: recover one time, date, schedule, appointment, or near-future
  span from an unambiguous full cue.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–9.
- Exclusions: future simple, past calendars, timetable dictation, and date-only substitution series.

### Slice 11 — `es-01-0401`–`es-01-0440`: food, drink, quantities, and preferences

- Grammar and construction burden: articles and noun agreement; present `comer`, `tomar`,
  `preferir`, and `gustar`; `me/te/le gusta` only as a high-frequency preference pattern; negation
  and quantity expressions.
- Communicative function: name ordinary foods and drinks, state preferences, ask what someone likes,
  choose a quantity, and describe a simple meal.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: Mexican meals, tortillas, fruit, water, coffee, `jugo`, menu
  items, `me gusta`, `prefiero`, `un poco de`, and `una botella de`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: Spanish `j` in `jugo`, tap in `prefiero`, lexical stress,
  phrase linking, and `gusta`/`gustan` number cues.
- Register and relationship context: home, host–guest, and ordinary neutral preference exchanges;
  avoid stereotypes about Mexican food.
- Listening and production targets: recover a preference construction, food noun phrase, article,
  agreement, or practical quantity chunk.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–10.
- Exclusions: recipes, cooking commands, indirect-object-pronoun expansion, and food dictionaries.

### Slice 12 — `es-01-0441`–`es-01-0480`: restaurant and café service

- Grammar and construction burden: service uses of `querer`, `poder`, `tener`, `traer`, and `dar`;
  articles, quantities, availability questions, choice, negation, and concise polite requests.
- Communicative function: request a table, order, ask about availability, add or correct one item,
  request the check, and complete payment.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: restaurant, café, table, menu, check, cash/card,
  `para llevar`, `la cuenta`, `¿Tiene...?`, and `¿Me trae...?` as bounded service chunks.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: connected courteous phrases, tap/trill, stable vowels,
  question contour, and canonical punctuation.
- Register and relationship context: binding customer–staff `usted`; staff uses respectful singular
  address; no service interaction uses `tú`.
- Listening and production targets: recover one useful order, availability, quantity, correction,
  payment, or service-request span from clear role context.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–11.
- Exclusions: commands, conditional mood, combined clitics, complaints beyond simple correction,
  and waiter caricature.

### Slice 13 — `es-01-0481`–`es-01-0520`: shopping, demonstratives, color, size, and price

- Grammar and construction burden: demonstrative adjectives and practical pronouns; adjective
  agreement and position; present `costar`, `quedar`, `parecer`, `tener`, and `llevar`; quantities,
  prices, and availability.
- Communicative function: identify an item, ask for a size or color, try a practical choice, compare
  only immediate options, check stock and price, choose, and pay.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: clothing, shoes, pharmacy and daily goods, colors, sizes,
  pesos, receipt, `este modelo`, `me queda`, and `pago con tarjeta`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: stress in demonstratives and color words, `ll/y` in `llevo`,
  linking around prices, and agreement endings.
- Register and relationship context: customer–staff `usted`; ordinary close-peer opinion only where
  the relationship is explicit.
- Listening and production targets: recover one demonstrative, agreement, fit, appearance,
  availability, price, or payment burden directly from the shopping situation.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–12.
- Exclusions: broad comparison systems, object-pronoun chains, bargaining scripts, and mechanical
  color/size tables.

### Slice 14 — `es-01-0521`–`es-01-0560`: transport, destination, directions, and travel time

- Grammar and construction burden: movement verbs `ir`, `salir`, `llegar`, `pasar`, `llevar`, and
  `venir`; `a`, `de`, `en`, `por` only in fixed route/location use, `hasta`, and place expressions;
  information questions.
- Communicative function: identify a destination, ask which city bus or metro to take, state a route,
  ask where to get off, and give approximate travel time.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: `camión` as city bus in clear context, metro, station, stop,
  `boleto`, street, corner, `¿Dónde pasa...?`, and `queda a ... minutos`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: stress in `camión`, Mexican `ll/y` in street vocabulary,
  linking in routes, and clear question rhythm.
- Register and relationship context: polite questions to staff or unfamiliar adults; neutral route
  coordination with peers.
- Listening and production targets: recover a transport noun, movement form, destination,
  directional phrase, or travel-time chunk from a complete route cue.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–13.
- Exclusions: productive broad `por/para`, travel narratives, commands, and exhaustive route lists.

### Slice 15 — `es-01-0561`–`es-01-0600`: weather, seasons, and simple activities

- Grammar and construction burden: weather uses of `hacer`, `estar`, and `hay`; present seasonal
  actions; adjective agreement; `cuando` only as a simple time link; short `pero` and `porque` links.
- Communicative function: state current weather, ask about conditions, choose clothing or a simple
  activity, and explain a basic weather-related choice.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: sun, rain, wind, heat, cold, seasons, umbrella, jacket,
  `hace calor`, `está nublado`, and `hay viento`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: silent `h`, Spanish `j`, stress in weather adjectives,
  ordinary linking, and natural exclamation contour where justified.
- Register and relationship context: neutral small talk and practical coordination; no regional
  weather caricature.
- Listening and production targets: distinguish `hacer`, `estar`, and `hay` weather patterns and
  recover a focused condition, clothing, activity, or reason span.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–14.
- Exclusions: forecasts with future tense, disaster language, complex conditionals, and seasonal
  noun substitution blocks.

### Slice 16 — `es-01-0601`–`es-01-0640`: immediate needs and simple everyday problems

- Grammar and construction burden: present `tener`, `necesitar`, `poder`, `querer`, `deber`,
  `encontrar`, and `funcionar`; negation; basic body-state and possession expressions.
- Communicative function: state hunger, thirst, tiredness, a minor symptom, a lost item, delay,
  broken everyday object, or need for simple non-specialist help.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: water, rest, bathroom, headache, keys, phone, charger,
  `no funciona`, `no encuentro`, `necesito ayuda`, and `tengo dolor de cabeza`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: diphthongs in high-frequency forms, Spanish `j`, stress,
  linking, and clear negative scope.
- Register and relationship context: direct close-peer help and polite requests to staff or an
  unfamiliar adult; no medical diagnosis.
- Listening and production targets: recover one need, state, missing object, malfunction, delay, or
  help span with enough visible context to identify the problem.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–15.
- Exclusions: medical advice, emergency procedures, commands, reflexive symptom systems, and past
  accident narration.

### Slice 17 — `es-01-0641`–`es-01-0680`: invitations, choices, and shared plans

- Grammar and construction burden: `querer`, `poder`, `preferir`, `ir a + infinitive`, and `quedar`;
  yes/no and information questions; `o`, `pero`, `porque`, `entonces`, `también`, and `tampoco`.
- Communicative function: invite, propose two practical choices, accept, decline tactfully with a
  simple reason, confirm a meeting point, and coordinate a concrete plan.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: café, park, movie, meal, study, time, meeting place,
  `¿Quieres...?`, `prefiero`, `quedamos en`, and `entonces nos vemos` as a fixed closing chunk.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: question and invitation contour, lexical stress, connected
  short links, and standard accents.
- Register and relationship context: mostly explicit close-peer `tú`; polite invitations use
  `usted` only where socially plausible.
- Listening and production targets: recover one invitation, preference, choice, reason, acceptance,
  refusal, or arrangement span from a coherent exchange.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–16.
- Exclusions: conditional invitations, productive reflexive systems, elaborate excuses, and
  mechanical venue substitutions.

### Slice 18 — `es-01-0681`–`es-01-0720`: object reference and routine transactions

- Grammar and construction burden: introductory direct-object `lo`, `la`, `los`, and `las` with one
  unmistakable visible referent; present `poner`, `dar`, `ver`, `traer`, `llevar`, and `pasar`;
  demonstratives and transaction prepositions.
- Communicative function: hand over, place, bring, take, see, or check a named object; refer back to
  one purchase, document, ticket, key, or item in a short service exchange.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: identification, receipt, ticket, bag, document, keys,
  counter, `lo veo`, `la llevo`, `los tiene`, and `las pongo aquí` with explicit antecedents.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: clitic linking without spelling change, `ll/y`, tap/trill,
  sentence stress on the action or object, and punctuation boundaries.
- Register and relationship context: polite staff/customer and neutral household exchanges with
  unambiguous role ownership.
- Listening and production targets: recover one direct-object reference or transaction verb phrase
  while the referent remains clearly visible outside the cloze.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–17.
- Exclusions: indirect objects, combined clitics, clitic climbing, commands, `se`, and pronoun
  paradigm tables.

### Slice 19 — `es-01-0721`–`es-01-0760`: plural address, courtesy, confirmation, and repair

- Grammar and construction burden: practical `ustedes` with useful present forms; plural noun and
  adjective agreement; question forms; negation; present `decir`, `saber`, `conocer`, `poder`, and
  `tener`; concise courtesy and repair chunks.
- Communicative function: address two or more people, welcome or orient a group, apologize, thank,
  confirm a shared detail, request repetition, and repair a simple misunderstanding.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: family groups, customers, classmates, visitors, reservations,
  schedules, `¿Ustedes tienen...?`, `muchas gracias`, `disculpe`, and `¿Puede repetir?`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: plural verb and noun endings, natural `ustedes` rhythm,
  question contour, stress, and inverted punctuation.
- Register and relationship context: `ustedes` is the only plural address; singular `usted` remains
  distinct; productive `vosotros` and `vos` are prohibited.
- Listening and production targets: identify plural addressees and recover a group-directed verb,
  agreement, confirmation, apology, thanks, or repair span.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–18.
- Exclusions: Spanish plural-address comparison lessons, commands, formal speeches, and person or
  number paradigm grids.

### Slice 20 — `es-01-0761`–`es-01-0800`: integrated practical A1 interaction

- Grammar and construction burden: integrated use of stage-approved articles, agreement, present
  verbs, `ser`/`estar`/`hay`/`tener`, questions, negation, possessives, demonstratives, modals,
  introductory direct objects, concrete plans, prepositions, numbers, and short connectors.
- Communicative function: complete short realistic A1 exchanges that combine identity, home,
  routine, food, shopping, transport, schedule, weather, need, invitation, and repair skills without
  adding a new grammatical system.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: cross-domain combinations such as confirming a reservation
  and time, choosing an item and payment, arranging transport, or repairing one contact detail.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: connected Mexican Spanish rhythm, stable vowels, stress,
  tap/trill, `ll/y`, Spanish `j`, `ñ`, linking, accents, and canonical punctuation.
- Register and relationship context: consistent `tú`, `usted`, or `ustedes` based on explicit roles;
  no unexplained register switch.
- Listening and production targets: recover one focused practical span that carries the integrated
  burden while meaningful context remains visible.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–19.
- Exclusions: any Mexican Spanish 02 structure, synthetic capstone summaries, long dictation,
  repeated templates, and internal stage or review language.
