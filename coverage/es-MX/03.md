# Mexican Spanish 03 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

- Locale and production model: `es-MX`, educated contemporary central Mexican Spanish.
- Canonical learner input: NFC Spanish with standard accents, `ñ`, inverted question and
  exclamation marks, and canonical punctuation.
- Planned and justified size: exactly 1,400 original cards, `es-03-0001` through
  `es-03-1400`.
- Structure: thirty-five independent 40-card slices. Slices 1–12 form audit partition 1,
  slices 13–24 form partition 2, and slices 25–35 form partition 3.
- Address model: singular `tú` for explicit close relationships; singular `usted` for unfamiliar
  adults, staff, and polite service; `ustedes` for every plural-address context.
- Immutable prerequisites: all accepted Mexican Spanish 01–02 cards and primary targets. Each
  later slice also requires all earlier accepted Mexican Spanish 03 slices and targets.

Every card tests one focused, normalized-unique production span in a complete natural sentence or
short coherent exchange. Its assigned construction must occur inside the cloze. Whole-sentence
targets are limited to compact reusable fixed expressions. A noun, name, date, number, person,
tense, or arbitrary larger span does not create distinct coverage.

## Binding stage-wide scope

- Build useful B1 control of connected narration, past perfect, productive present subjunctive,
  future and conditional forms, practical hypotheses, clitic and pronominal reference, reported
  speech, relatives, cohesion, opinion, negotiation, and interaction repair.
- Preserve the ordinary Mexican preference for the simple preterite in completed periods. Use the
  present perfect only for current relevance or open periods.
- Keep every subjunctive trigger visible and its assigned form inside the target. Do not create
  conjugation tables, trigger substitutions, or targets enlarged only to avoid duplication.
- Use clear antecedents for clitics, relatives, dislocation, and `se`. Do not teach difficult
  stacking, ethical datives, or long-distance discourse reference.
- Use contemporary Mexican vocabulary and stable relationship context. Productive `vosotros` and
  `vos`, unexplained slang, regional caricature, and Peninsular tense defaults are prohibited.
- Recycle natural Mexican rhythm, lexical and grammatical stress, tap/trill distinctions,
  Mexican `ll/y`, Spanish `j`, `ñ`, connected clitics, consonant linking, and appropriate
  narrative, courteous, disagreement, repair, and confirmation contours.
- Do not create translated textbook prose, dictionary blocks, paradigm tables, mechanical
  substitutions, connector-plus-subject fragments, antecedent-plus-relative padding, artificial
  metadata, specialist advice, or process language as learner content.

### Mexican Spanish 04 boundary

Reserve imperfect, perfect, and pluperfect subjunctive; future and conditional perfect;
counterfactual past; advanced tense backshift; sustained multi-timeline or literary narration;
formal `cuyo` and `el cual` systems; complex relative chains; advanced reported narration;
sustained abstract argumentation; and subtle stance contrasts for Mexican Spanish 04 or later.

## Slice plan and binding burdens

### Slice 1 — `es-03-0001`–`es-03-0040`: connected preterite and imperfect narration

- Grammar and construction burden: controlled preterite/imperfect contrast for foreground events,
  background states, descriptions, and repeated activity in short connected accounts.
- Communicative function: recount a trip, celebration, workday, study experience, or ordinary
  setback while distinguishing what happened from what was already in progress.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: travel, work, study, relationships, errands, weather,
  `mientras`, `cuando`, `de repente`, and `en ese momento`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: preterite final stress, imperfect endings, linking, and
  natural narrative contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral personal or collegial narration with stable address.
- Listening and production targets: produce one tense-marked narrative span from visible temporal
  and event context.
- Prerequisites: Mexican Spanish 01–02.
- Exclusions: past perfect, literary narration, tense tables, and sustained multi-timeline stories.

### Slice 2 — `es-03-0041`–`es-03-0080`: interruption, chronology, and past-time links

- Grammar and construction burden: `cuando`, `mientras`, `al + infinitivo`, `antes de`,
  `después de`, `hasta que`, and `en cuanto` with transparent past reference.
- Communicative function: order events, mark interruption, and explain timing in travel, services,
  housing, work, and social plans.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: arrivals, calls, meetings, deliveries, repairs, routes,
  `al llegar`, `mientras esperaba`, and `en cuanto terminó`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: linked time phrases, preterite/imperfect stress, and pause
  placement in connected narration.
- Register and relationship context: neutral explanation to a friend, colleague, or service worker.
- Listening and production targets: recover one time-link construction that directly organizes a
  short past account.
- Prerequisites: slice 1 and Mexican Spanish 01–02.
- Exclusions: advanced backshift, future-time subjunctive links, and isolated connector fragments.

### Slice 3 — `es-03-0081`–`es-03-0120`: introductory past perfect

- Grammar and construction burden: `había + participio` for an event completed before one clear
  past reference point, including common irregular participles.
- Communicative function: explain a missed connection, changed plan, work problem, forgotten task,
  lost item, or discovery by naming what had already happened.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: reservations, messages, files, deliveries, preparation,
  `ya había`, `todavía no había`, and `nunca había`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: imperfect stress in `había`, participle rhythm, and written
  accents.
- Register and relationship context: neutral personal, professional, and service explanations.
- Listening and production targets: produce a focused anterior-event span with the later past event
  visible.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–2 and Mexican Spanish 01–02.
- Exclusions: pluperfect subjunctive, complex backshift, and multiple competing past reference times.

### Slice 4 — `es-03-0121`–`es-03-0160`: current relevance and open periods

- Grammar and construction burden: limited present perfect for current relevance, unfinished time,
  recent results, and life experience, contrasted naturally with Mexican simple preterite use.
- Communicative function: report progress, recent changes, completed tasks in an open period, and
  experiences relevant to a current decision.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: work, study, technology, travel, habits, `esta semana`,
  `hasta ahora`, `ya he`, and `todavía no he`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: auxiliary-participle linking, participle stress, and question
  contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral personal or workplace updates.
- Listening and production targets: choose and produce a present-perfect span only when current
  relevance or an open period is explicit.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–3 and Mexican Spanish 01–02.
- Exclusions: Peninsular completed-today defaults, perfect subjunctive, and compound-tense tables.

### Slice 5 — `es-03-0161`–`es-03-0200`: duration, repetition, completion, and change

- Grammar and construction burden: `llevar + gerundio`, `seguir + gerundio`, `dejar de`,
  `volver a`, `acabar de`, `estar a punto de`, and `desde que` in clear time frames.
- Communicative function: describe continuing progress, stopped habits, repeated attempts, recent
  completion, and imminent actions.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: training, projects, devices, routines, savings, repairs,
  learning goals, and schedule changes.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: connected periphrases, gerund stress, and time-span rhythm.
- Register and relationship context: personal, collegial, and practical service updates.
- Listening and production targets: produce one aspectual span that matches the visible phase or
  duration of the action.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–4 and Mexican Spanish 01–02.
- Exclusions: mechanical aspect substitutions, advanced aspect theory, and future perfect.

### Slice 6 — `es-03-0201`–`es-03-0240`: regular present subjunctive for influence

- Grammar and construction burden: productive regular present-subjunctive forms after requests,
  advice, recommendations, preferences, desire, and necessity.
- Communicative function: ask, advise, recommend, prefer, or require that another person act in a
  practical work, study, home, travel, or event context.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: schedules, files, preparation, reservations, maintenance,
  `quiero que`, `te recomiendo que`, and `es necesario que`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: subjunctive vowel contrasts, person endings, linking, and
  courteous influence contour.
- Register and relationship context: explicit `tú`, `usted`, or `ustedes` relationships remain
  stable.
- Listening and production targets: produce the required regular subjunctive form while its trigger
  remains visible.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–5 and Mexican Spanish 01–02.
- Exclusions: trigger-only targets, conjugation tables, and all past or perfect subjunctive forms.

- Prerequisites: slice 6 and Mexican Spanish 01–02.
- Exclusions: arbitrary person paradigms, perfect subjunctive, and uncertain trigger semantics.
