# Mexican Spanish 02 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

- Locale and production model: `es-MX`, educated contemporary central Mexican Spanish.
- Canonical learner input: NFC Spanish with standard accents, `ñ`, inverted question and
  exclamation marks, and canonical punctuation.
- Planned and justified size: exactly 1,000 original cards, `es-02-0001` through `es-02-1000`.
- Structure: twenty-five independent 40-card slices. The first ten slices form audit partition 1,
  the next ten form partition 2, and the final five form partition 3.
- Address model: singular `tú` for explicit close relationships; singular `usted` for unfamiliar
  adults, staff, and polite service; `ustedes` for every plural-address context.
- Immutable prerequisite: all accepted Mexican Spanish 01 cards and primary targets. Each later
  slice also requires all earlier accepted Mexican Spanish 02 slices and their target inventory.

Every card tests one focused, normalized-unique production span in a complete natural sentence or
short coherent exchange. Its assigned construction must occur inside the cloze. Whole-sentence
targets are limited to compact reusable fixed expressions. A name, noun, number, date, gender,
person, tense, or larger arbitrary span does not create new coverage.

## Binding stage-wide scope

- Build useful A2 control of past time, object and reflexive reference, commands, progress and
  aspect, plans and practical future, core `por` and `para`, comparison, short relatives,
  indefinite and negative expressions, sentence links, quantities, and practical prepositions.
- Prefer the simple preterite for ordinary completed recent events in Mexican Spanish. Use the
  present perfect only for current relevance or an open time period.
- Keep tense contrasts bounded and transparent. Sustained narrative management, past perfect,
  literary narration, counterfactual past, and reported narration remain outside the stage.
- Keep clitic combinations transparent. Difficult stacking, accidental `se`, ethical datives,
  advanced doubling contrasts, and discourse-level reference remain outside the stage.
- Negative commands may introduce their required forms without opening the productive
  present-subjunctive system. Broad conditional and hypothetical discourse remain outside.
- Use contemporary Mexican vocabulary and stable relationship context. Do not use productive
  `vosotros` or `vos`, Peninsular recent-past defaults, unexplained slang, or regional caricature.
- Recycle natural stress, vowels, tap/trill contrasts, Mexican `ll/y`, Spanish `j`, `ñ`, consonant
  linking, connected clitic groups, and appropriate question, warning, narrative, and confirmation
  contours without phonetic respelling or IPA.
- Do not create dictionary-entry blocks, translated textbook prose, paradigm tables, mechanical
  substitutions, artificial connector fragments, relative-pronoun padding, or arbitrary target
  enlargement.

## Slice plan and binding burdens

### Slice 1 — `es-02-0001`–`es-02-0040`: completed personal events in the preterite

- Grammar and construction burden: regular preterite forms and high-frequency completed forms of
  `ser`, `ir`, `estar`, `tener`, and `hacer`; explicit finished-time anchors.
- Communicative function: recount one completed visit, errand, meal, meeting, celebration, or
  ordinary personal event and ask a concise follow-up question.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: yesterday, last weekend, appointments, errands, meals,
  visits, `fui a`, `estuve en`, `tuve que`, and `hice una`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: preterite final stress, accents in `fui` contexts and regular
  first/third-person forms, tap/trill distinctions, and short narrative contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral personal exchange; explicit close-peer `tú` and
  polite `usted` remain stable within each cue.
- Listening and production targets: produce one completed-event form or time-bounded preterite
  chunk directly from visible event context.
- Prerequisites: all accepted Mexican Spanish 01 source.
- Exclusions: imperfect background, multi-event stories, person-by-person conjugation tables, and
  present-perfect substitution.

### Slice 2 — `es-02-0041`–`es-02-0080`: irregular preterite in trips and practical errands

- Grammar and construction burden: useful preterites of `poder`, `poner`, `venir`, `decir`, `dar`,
  and `ver`, plus spelling changes in `llegar` and other matrix-justified common verbs.
- Communicative function: report what happened during transport, shopping, a delivery, a visit, or
  a simple service problem.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: station, route, ticket, package, counter, receipt, `no pude`,
  `puse`, `vino`, `me dijo`, `di`, `vi`, and `llegué`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: irregular preterite stress, written accents, Spanish `j`,
  linking around object groups, and completed-event contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral narration to a peer and courteous reports to service
  staff without changing address forms mid-cue.
- Listening and production targets: recover one distinctive irregular past form or useful completed
  collocation from a clear practical outcome.
- Prerequisites: slice 1 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: commands, combined clitics, imperfect contrast, and mechanical verb paradigms.

### Slice 3 — `es-02-0081`–`es-02-0120`: past decisions, knowledge, work, and study events

- Grammar and construction burden: useful preterites of `saber`, `querer`, `traer`, `decir`,
  `hacer`, and other justified irregulars, with meaning-sensitive past uses such as finding out or
  attempting in unambiguous contexts.
- Communicative function: explain a completed work or study task, an appointment outcome, a changed
  decision, or how a routine problem was resolved.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: class, training, assignment, meeting, file, appointment,
  `supe que`, `quise`, `traje`, `dije`, and `hice el trámite`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: stress in irregular stems, `j` in `traje` and `dije`, and
  natural focus on the outcome.
- Register and relationship context: neutral colleagues and classmates; polite institutional
  interaction when `usted` is explicit.
- Listening and production targets: produce a meaning-appropriate irregular preterite span rather
  than translate an isolated verb.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–2 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: reported narration, sustained stories, advanced aspect, and synonym lists.

### Slice 4 — `es-02-0121`–`es-02-0160`: introductory imperfect for past circumstances

- Grammar and construction burden: imperfect forms for age, clock time, weather, physical or
  emotional state, location, and description; high-frequency `era`, `estaba`, `había`, and `tenía`.
- Communicative function: describe how a person, place, day, room, or situation was at an earlier
  time and supply necessary background.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: childhood age, old neighborhood, weather, hotel room,
  workplace, `cuando tenía`, `era`, `estaba`, and `había`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: imperfect stress and written accents, contrast with
  preterite final stress, stable vowels, and descriptive contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral recollection without intrusive personal detail.
- Listening and production targets: recover one background-state or descriptive imperfect span
  from a clearly past frame.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–3 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: completed-event targets, habitual sequences, literary description, and tense tables.

### Slice 5 — `es-02-0161`–`es-02-0200`: earlier habits and repeated background activity

- Grammar and construction burden: imperfect for repeated or habitual past actions with frequency
  expressions and childhood or earlier-routine frames.
- Communicative function: describe former routines in school, work, home, transport, leisure, and
  family life at introductory depth.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: `antes`, `de niño/a`, `todos los veranos`, `cada semana`,
  commuting, chores, study, games, and visits.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: `-aba` and `-ía` stress, accents in `ía`, linking with
  frequency chunks, and habitual narrative rhythm.
- Register and relationship context: neutral personal reminiscence and respectful questions about
  another person's earlier routine.
- Listening and production targets: produce one habitual imperfect construction with meaningful
  frequency or period context.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–4 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: mechanical childhood substitutions, sustained biography, present habits, and past
  perfect.

### Slice 6 — `es-02-0201`–`es-02-0240`: bounded preterite and imperfect contrast

- Grammar and construction burden: one completed event against a visible background state or
  habitual activity; `cuando` and `mientras` in short transparent accounts.
- Communicative function: explain a delay, interruption, discovery, minor mishap, arrival, or
  changed plan with minimal necessary background.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: traffic, weather, phone calls, appointments, work, home,
  `mientras ...`, `cuando ...`, and one clear outcome.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: audible stress contrast between preterite and imperfect,
  linking across `cuando` or `mientras`, and event-focus contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral practical explanations and concise apologies.
- Listening and production targets: choose and produce the aspectually correct focused span while
  the contrasting context remains visible.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–5 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: long narratives, multiple competing timelines, past perfect, and causal speculation.

### Slice 7 — `es-02-0241`–`es-02-0280`: current relevance, recent past, and chronology

- Grammar and construction burden: limited present perfect for current relevance or open periods;
  Mexican preterite for finished periods; `acabar de + infinitive`; `primero`, `luego`, `después`,
  `antes de`, `durante`, `desde`, `hace`, and `desde hace`.
- Communicative function: report a just-completed action, current experience, recent update, or
  short ordered sequence and state how long a situation has continued.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: messages, tasks, visits, repairs, study, travel updates,
  `acabo de`, `esta semana he`, `ayer`, `desde hace`, and `hace ... que`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: participle stress, `he` without accent, connected chronology
  chunks, and natural update contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral updates among peers, colleagues, relatives, and
  courteous service contacts.
- Listening and production targets: produce the time-sensitive form or chronology chunk justified
  by the visible open or completed period.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–6 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: Peninsular recent-past defaults, broad compound-tense systems, and sustained accounts.

### Slice 8 — `es-02-0281`–`es-02-0320`: productive direct-object pronouns

- Grammar and construction burden: `lo`, `la`, `los`, and `las` before finite verbs in clear
  present, preterite, and practical future contexts, plus placement with one infinitive.
- Communicative function: confirm, locate, use, return, exchange, send, open, or replace a clearly
  identified item without needless repetition.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: purchases, receipts, tickets, keys, files, messages,
  documents, and household objects.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: connected pronoun-verb groups, plural `s`, Mexican `ll/y`,
  and contrastive confirmation contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral practical exchange with unmistakable antecedents.
- Listening and production targets: produce a complete but focused direct-object pronoun group in
  the tense required by visible context.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–7 and introductory direct-object exposure in Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: arbitrary noun/location enlargement, pronoun paradigms, doubling contrasts, and
  combined clitics.

### Slice 9 — `es-02-0321`–`es-02-0360`: indirect-object recipients and experiencers

- Grammar and construction burden: productive `me`, `te`, `le`, `nos`, and `les` for recipients,
  beneficiaries, and common experiencer patterns, including ordinary clarifying doubling.
- Communicative function: say who receives information, help, an item, or a service and describe
  ordinary reactions or needs.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: messages, gifts, instructions, deliveries, favors, work
  tasks, `le mandé`, `nos dieron`, `a ... le`, and common experiencer verbs.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: unstressed clitic linking, clear plural `les`, tap/trill
  distinctions, and recipient focus.
- Register and relationship context: stable `tú`, `usted`, or third-person reference based on the
  declared relationship.
- Listening and production targets: produce one natural indirect-object group with a clear semantic
  role rather than infer from an isolated noun.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–8 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: ethical datives, redundant mechanical doubling, combined clitics, and diagnosis or
  treatment advice.

### Slice 10 — `es-02-0361`–`es-02-0400`: transparent combined clitics

- Grammar and construction burden: useful `se lo`, `se la`, `se los`, and `se las` before finite
  verbs and in one transparent two-verb or affirmative-command context.
- Communicative function: confirm giving, sending, showing, explaining, returning, or delivering a
  clearly identified item to a stated recipient.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: receipt, form, key, tickets, photos, files, instructions,
  counter service, and deliveries.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: connected two-clitic groups, plural endings, statement and
  confirmation contours, and standard written spacing.
- Register and relationship context: neutral explanations and courteous transactions with explicit
  recipients and referents.
- Listening and production targets: produce one semantically transparent combined group without
  hiding the entire proposition.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–9 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: difficult stacking, accidental `se`, arbitrary gender/number tables, and discourse-
  level reference tracking.

### Slice 11 — `es-02-0401`–`es-02-0440`: reflexive routines, movement, and change of state

- Grammar and construction burden: common reflexive verbs for routines, preparation, movement,
  position, and ordinary change of state; finite and infinitive placement.
- Communicative function: describe getting ready, moving through a routine, settling in, becoming
  tired or calm, and preparing for an ordinary event.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: home, travel, work, study, celebrations, `levantarse`,
  `prepararse`, `sentarse`, `irse`, `quedarse`, and `ponerse` in transparent uses.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: connected reflexive groups, stress with attached infinitive
  pronouns, tap/trill distinctions, and routine rhythm.
- Register and relationship context: neutral self-description and relationship-supported questions
  without intrusive detail.
- Listening and production targets: produce one useful reflexive span whose subject and event are
  unambiguous.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–10 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: broad idiomatic `se`, accidental events, advanced change-of-state nuance, and routine
  substitution tables.

### Slice 12 — `es-02-0441`–`es-02-0480`: reciprocal, impersonal, and passive `se`

- Grammar and construction burden: reciprocal actions with clear plural subjects; introductory
  impersonal and passive `se` in signs, services, availability, instructions, and general facts.
- Communicative function: describe ordinary mutual interaction and understand or produce concise
  service rules, availability statements, and public instructions.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: classmates, colleagues, neighbors, store and transport
  signs, rentals, office services, `se ayudan`, `se vende`, `se buscan`, and `se permite`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: connected `se` groups, plural agreement in passive patterns,
  sign-reading rhythm, and instruction contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral third-person general statements and explicit plural
  participants; no ambiguous address shifts.
- Listening and production targets: distinguish reciprocal meaning from impersonal or passive
  service language and produce the assigned focused construction.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–11 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: accidental `se`, advanced passive alternation, vague agents, and artificial sign
  inventories.

### Slice 13 — `es-02-0481`–`es-02-0520`: affirmative `tú` commands and attached pronouns

- Grammar and construction burden: ordinary affirmative `tú` commands, including common irregular
  `ven`, `di`, `sal`, `haz`, `ten`, `ve`, `pon`, and `sé`, with transparent attached pronouns.
- Communicative function: give a friendly instruction, direction, reminder, invitation, or simple
  procedural step.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: home, food preparation, travel, devices, shared plans,
  `ven acá`, `haz clic`, `ponlo`, `dime`, and `ten cuidado`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: command stress, written accents created by attachment,
  connected clitic delivery, and friendly instruction contour.
- Register and relationship context: explicit close peer, relative, partner, or child; never use
  `tú` commands with unfamiliar service staff.
- Listening and production targets: produce one natural command or command-plus-pronoun span with
  meaningful action context visible.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–12 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: negative commands, command paradigms, harsh unsupported imperatives, and broad
  subjunctive production.

### Slice 14 — `es-02-0521`–`es-02-0560`: negative and polite commands

- Grammar and construction burden: ordinary negative `tú` commands; affirmative and negative
  `usted` and `ustedes` commands; correct pre-verbal pronoun placement in negatives.
- Communicative function: give a warning, polite instruction, service direction, safety reminder,
  or group request and soften it where context requires.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: transport, workplace, classroom, home, public spaces,
  `no olvides`, `no lo mueva`, `pasen`, and `no se preocupen`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: negative-command stress, connected pronouns, plural command
  endings, warning and courteous instruction contours, and accents where required.
- Register and relationship context: explicit `tú`, `usted`, or `ustedes` relationship remains
  stable and pragmatically appropriate.
- Listening and production targets: produce the requested negative or polite command itself, not an
  unrelated location or time adjunct.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–13 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: productive broad subjunctive, medical or safety expertise, legal rules, and paradigm
  tables.

### Slice 15 — `es-02-0561`–`es-02-0600`: actions genuinely in progress

- Grammar and construction burden: present progressive `estar + gerundio` for actions happening
  now, including transparent clitic placement before the finite verb or attached to the gerund.
- Communicative function: say what someone is doing now, explain a current delay, give a live work
  or travel update, and ask about an ongoing action.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: calls, messages, cooking, commuting, loading, studying,
  device updates, `estoy esperando`, and `lo estoy buscando`/`estoy buscándolo`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: gerund stress, written accents after pronoun attachment,
  linking within the verb group, and live-update contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral current-status exchange with peers, colleagues,
  relatives, or service contacts.
- Listening and production targets: produce one genuinely progressive action span with a visible
  now-context.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–14 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: using the progressive for simple habits, mechanical placement pairs, past
  progressive, and advanced aspect.

### Slice 16 — `es-02-0601`–`es-02-0640`: aspectual patterns and practical continuation

- Grammar and construction burden: high-frequency `empezar a`, `seguir + gerundio`, `dejar de`,
  `volver a`, and `acabar de` in present or one clearly justified past context.
- Communicative function: report starting, continuing, stopping, repeating, or just completing a
  task, habit, repair, course, or ordinary activity.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: work tasks, study, exercise, repairs, device behavior,
  travel routines, and household activities.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: stem-change stress, gerund linking, infinitive groups,
  Spanish `j`, and aspectual focus.
- Register and relationship context: neutral coordination and personal updates; concise polite
  status reports where appropriate.
- Listening and production targets: produce the aspectual construction itself with enough visible
  event context to distinguish its meaning.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–15 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: lexical substitution blocks, advanced aspectual nuance, passive periphrases, and
  sustained narratives.

### Slice 17 — `es-02-0641`–`es-02-0680`: plans, practical future, promises, and uncertainty

- Grammar and construction burden: extension of `ir a`, `tener que`, `deber`, `poder`, and
  `querer`; practical future simple for intentions, arrangements, promises, schedules,
  predictions, and ordinary uncertainty; fixed polite conditional chunks only.
- Communicative function: arrange, cancel, reschedule, promise, predict, state a practical future
  obligation, and make a softened request.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: appointments, invitations, travel, work deadlines, weather,
  deliveries, `te llamaré`, `llegará`, `tendré que`, `podría ayudarme`, and `quisiera`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: future stress and accents, irregular future stems, question
  contour in polite chunks, and connected planning rhythm.
- Register and relationship context: plans among peers; polite fixed requests with unfamiliar
  staff; no relationship shift.
- Listening and production targets: produce the practical future or fixed softened-request span
  directly required by the visible communicative purpose.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–16 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: productive conditional paradigms, hypothetical discourse, future-perfect forms, and
  arbitrary date substitutions.

### Slice 18 — `es-02-0681`–`es-02-0720`: core uses of `por`

- Grammar and construction burden: `por` for route, exchange, cause, duration, means, and ordinary
  frequency in natural fixed or productive A2 patterns.
- Communicative function: explain a route or reason, state a price or exchange, describe duration
  or frequency, and identify a communication or transport means.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: streets, transit, payment, phone and email, delays, rentals,
  `por aquí`, `por veinte pesos`, `por la lluvia`, `por dos días`, and `por semana`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: tap in `por`, linking into articles and quantities, sentence
  stress on reasons or routes, and natural explanatory contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral practical explanation and courteous transaction.
- Listening and production targets: produce the appropriate core `por` span from a context that
  rules out an unrelated use.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–17 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: `para` contrast drills without communicative purpose, advanced causal nuance,
  idiomatic inventories, and mechanical preposition tables.

### Slice 19 — `es-02-0721`–`es-02-0760`: core uses of `para` and controlled contrast

- Grammar and construction burden: `para` for destination, recipient, purpose, deadline, intended
  use, and viewpoint, with limited natural `por`/`para` contrasts.
- Communicative function: state where something goes, who it is for, why it is needed, when it is
  due, how it should be used, or a simple viewpoint.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: travel, gifts, forms, work, study, tools, appointments,
  `para mañana`, `para usted`, `para llegar`, and `para mí`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: linking around `para`, stress in infinitive purpose groups,
  recipient focus, and contrastive confirmation contour.
- Register and relationship context: neutral and polite practical interaction with stable address.
- Listening and production targets: produce one justified `para` span or a bounded contrast whose
  meaning is clear from visible context.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–18 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: advanced viewpoint nuance, broad essay-like contrast, idiom lists, and minimal-pair
  tables.

### Slice 20 — `es-02-0761`–`es-02-0800`: comparisons and practical choices

- Grammar and construction burden: comparisons and superlatives with adjectives, adverbs, nouns,

- Communicative function: compare options, quantities, prices, travel times, housing, products, or
  routines and make a simple justified choice or recommendation.
- Lexical domains and reusable chunks: stores, rooms, routes, devices, courses, meals, `más ... que`,
  `tan ... como`, `el mejor`, `cuesta menos`, and `hay tantos`.
- Pronunciation or orthography burden: comparative phrase stress, linking before `que` and `como`,

- Register and relationship context: neutral discussion and polite service comparison.
- Listening and production targets: produce the complete comparison burden while keeping both
  compared items or the choice context visible.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–19 and Mexican Spanish 01.
- Exclusions: gender/number comparison tables, unsupported value judgments, advanced degree
  modifiers, and abstract argumentation.

### Slice 21 — `es-02-0801`–`es-02-0840`: short relatives and indefinite reference

- Grammar and construction burden: short useful relative clauses with `que` and `donde`; ordinary
  `algo`, `nada`, `alguien`, `nadie`, `algún`, and `ningún` with required agreement.
- Communicative function: identify a person, object, file, place, or service; report whether an
  appropriate item or person exists; clarify lost property or technology needs.

- Prerequisites: slices 1–20 and Mexican Spanish 01.

### Slice 22 — `es-02-0841`–`es-02-0880`: reasons, results, sequence, contrast, and condition

- Grammar and construction burden: high-frequency cause, consequence, purpose, sequence,

### Slice 23 — `es-02-0881`–`es-02-0920`: practical prepositions, quantities, and estimates
