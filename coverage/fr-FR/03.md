# French 03 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

- Locale and production model: `fr-FR`, contemporary metropolitan French at practical B1.
- Canonical learner input: NFC French spelling with standard accents and ASCII apostrophe U+0027.
- Planned size: exactly 1,400 original cards, `fr-03-0001` through `fr-03-1400`.
- Structure: thirty-five independent 40-card slices. Completion depends on the binding burdens and
  cumulative reviews below, not on reaching a numerical ceiling.
- Immutable prerequisite: all 1,800 accepted French 01–02 cards and targets. Each French 03 slice
  may use French 01–02 and accepted earlier French 03 slices as support, never same-slice or later
  language.
- Default relationship model: `vous` for unfamiliar adults, professional contacts, and service
  interactions; `tu` only with an explicitly identified close relationship.

Every card tests one focused, normalized-unique production span in a complete natural sentence or
short coherent exchange. A target never hides a complete independent or dependent proposition
unless it is a compact reusable fixed expression or compact document field. Visible context must
remain meaningful; punctuation, a connector, a subject, or a politeness frame alone is not enough.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Systematic practical contrast among `passé composé` and `imparfait`, common
  `plus-que-parfait`, and chronological control in connected narration.
- Present, near future, future simple, and conditional present for plans, predictions,
  consequences, advice, preferences, probability, and ordinary hypotheses.
- Practical real and present-hypothesis `si` clauses without advanced counterfactual sequencing.
- Frequent present-subjunctive forms after necessity, desire, emotion, doubt, judgment, purpose,
  and concession; transparent infinitive-versus-subjunctive choices based on subject continuity.
- Cause, consequence, purpose, opposition, concession, restriction, and ordinary subordination.
- Direct and indirect objects, common two-pronoun groups, productive `y` and `en`, reflexive and
  reciprocal constructions, and ordinary pronoun order with finite verbs, auxiliaries,
  infinitives, and imperatives.
- Common past-participle agreement with `être`, reflexive verbs, and a preceding direct object in
  clear high-frequency contexts.
- Productive `qui`, `que`, `où`, `dont`, frequent preposition-plus-relative patterns, and reported
  statements, questions, and instructions with ordinary person and tense adjustment.

Literary tenses, advanced tense concordance, dense pronoun chains, difficult agreement edge cases,
rare subjunctive triggers, advanced relatives, and sustained rhetorical subordination are reserved
for French 04 or later.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Narrate experiences, problems, decisions, and changes with clear event sequence and background.
- Explain causes and consequences; express probability, uncertainty, wishes, regret, obligation,
  permission, advice, preferences, and practical hypotheses.
- Give and support opinions with examples, compare options, and make calibrated recommendations.
- Handle travel, accommodation, transport, scheduling, disruptions, complaints, housing, repairs,
  neighborhood matters, shopping, returns, services, and lost property independently.
- Coordinate work, study, training, projects, deadlines, and ordinary professional interaction.
- Discuss familiar social, cultural, environmental, media, technology, and community topics.
- State practical health needs without diagnosis or medical advice.
- Summarize a short podcast, interview, article, or presentation and produce ordinary formal
  messages, complaints, requests, explanations, and reviews.
- Clarify, reformulate, interrupt politely, correct misunderstandings, and maintain a conversation.

## 3. Core lexical domains

- Experiences, memories, event sequence, change, habits, decisions, causes, and consequences.
- Travel, accommodation, routes, schedules, disruptions, refunds, complaints, and independent
  problem resolution.
- Housing, repairs, neighborhood coordination, utilities, services, lost property, and purchases.
- Work, study, training, projects, deadlines, meetings, responsibilities, and routine formal mail.
- Health and practical needs, leisure, culture, books, films, media, technology, environment,
  community life, and familiar social questions.
- Opinions, evidence, examples, probability, uncertainty, recommendations, summaries, and repair.

## 4. Collocations and reusable chunks

Coverage favors ordinary combinations such as `je m'étais rendu compte`, `il faudrait vérifier`,
`à condition que`, `bien que`, `afin de`, `avoir beau`, `en tenir compte`, `s'en occuper`,
`le document dont j'ai besoin`, `ce à quoi je pense`, `elle m'a demandé de`, `d'après`,
`en revanche`, `autrement dit`, `si j'étais à votre place`, and domain-specific chunks whose
force is recoverable from the complete cue. Dictionary series, conjugation tables, mechanical
substitutions, and translated textbook prose do not count.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- Standard accented NFC spelling and ASCII apostrophe U+0027; no IPA or phonetic respelling.
- Stable vowel and nasal-vowel contrasts, French `r`, elision, enchaînement, high-frequency liaison,
  prohibited liaison, and ordinary schwa behavior.
- Connected pronoun, auxiliary, negative, relative, and subordinate groups; recoverable tense,
  mood, and agreement endings.
- Rhythmic groups, final stress, narrative and explanation contour, question and instruction
  contour, and turn-management prosody.
- Canonical accents, agreement, apostrophes, hyphens, capitalization, and punctuation.

## 6. Register and relationship context

- `Vous` is binding for unfamiliar adults, staff, professional contacts, and service interaction.
- `Tu` is binding only where a close relationship is explicit in the sentence or concise support.
- One interaction never switches speech level without an explicit relationship change.
- Requests, disagreement, complaints, interruption, advice, and correction are softened to match
  role and setting; professional coordination states ownership without management jargon.
- Use ordinary contemporary metropolitan French, not regional production, marked slang, literary
  archaism, caricature, or translation-like wording.

## 7. Pragmatics

- Narration distinguishes background, completed events, and prior events without hidden facts.
- Opinions are supported by a visible example or reason and remain calibrated to the evidence.
- Pronouns and relatives have recoverable antecedents; reported speech preserves speaker and force.
- Requests, refusals, complaints, apologies, advice, and interruptions match the relationship.
- Summaries distinguish source content from the speaker's interpretation.
- Repair language identifies the uncertain, corrected, or reformulated detail.

## 8. Listening targets

- Recover one focused canonical span from connected B1 narration, explanation, summary, or dialogue.
- Distinguish tense, mood, polarity, pronoun order, agreement, reported force, and referents.
- Resolve connected auxiliaries, pronouns, relatives, liaison, enchaînement, schwa, and silent
  endings from context without phonetic support.
- Track chronology, cause, consequence, concession, condition, and topic continuity across a short
  coherent cue.
- Recover practical dates, times, quantities, comparisons, routes, conditions, and formal details.

## 9. Production targets

- Type one useful inflected form, pronoun group, collocation, connector, relative construction, or
  compact interactional chunk in canonical spelling.
- Select tense, mood, auxiliary, agreement, pronoun position, relative, reporting pattern,
  preposition, or connector from meaningful visible context.
- Produce practical narration, explanation, opinion, summary, service, professional, and repair
  language while maintaining relationship and register.
- Keep every target within one sentence, quotation, paragraph, and speaker turn, with terminal
  punctuation outside the target and meaningful context visible.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Every slice requires all 1,800 accepted French 01–02 cards and primary targets.
- Slice 1 has no French 03 prerequisite; slices 2–35 require every accepted earlier French 03 slice
  and its cumulative primary-target inventory.
- Same-slice and later-slice French 03 language cannot provide hidden support.
- Learners can follow one connected B1 cue and type one focused span without IPA or advanced
  linguistic terminology.

## 11. Exclusions

- Literary tenses, advanced tense sequencing, complex counterfactual discourse, advanced mood
  sequencing, and sustained stylistic narration.
- Dense multi-pronoun chains, difficult agreement edge cases, rare subjunctive triggers, advanced
  compound relatives, broad passive systems, and sustained formal argument.
- Specialist professional, legal, financial, diagnostic, or technical documents and advice.
- Regional production, marked slang, literary archaism, IPA, phonetic respelling, dictionary
  series, mechanical substitutions, internal process language, filler, and long dictation.
- French 04 cards or source, French bundle work, and any change to French 01–02.

## Slice plan and binding burdens

### Slice 1 — `fr-03-0001`–`fr-03-0040`: connected past narration and tense contrast

- Production burden: systematic practical contrast between completed events and background or
  habitual circumstances in short accounts of experiences, disruptions, and decisions.
- Register: neutral personal narration, close-peer experience sharing, and polite incident reports.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected auxiliary groups, imperfect endings, narrative rhythm, liaison,
  and enchaînement.
- Prerequisites: French 01–02 only.
- Excludes: pluperfect, sustained literary narration, hidden paragraph context, and long dictation.

### Slice 2 — `fr-03-0041`–`fr-03-0080`: common `plus-que-parfait`

- Production burden: one prior completed event relative to a clear past reference point, with
  common auxiliaries, negation, and ordinary agreement.
- Register: personal accounts, travel updates, workplace explanations, and service incidents.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected imperfect auxiliaries, participles, elision, and agreement.
- Prerequisites: French 01–02 and S01.
- Excludes: remote literary background, advanced tense concordance, and stacked prior events.

### Slice 3 — `fr-03-0081`–`fr-03-0120`: chronology and temporal clauses

- Pronunciation/spelling: clause grouping, liaison, temporal rhythm, accents, and apostrophes.
- Prerequisites: French 01–02 and S01–S02.
- Excludes: literary connectors, advanced tense sequencing, and ambiguous event order.

## Slice acceptance workflow

For every slice, a fresh author process returns exactly the assigned 40 cards from the binding
matrix and cumulative accepted-target index. A separate fresh metropolitan-French editor returns a
fully corrected 40-card JSON batch. The executor accepts only a batch that passes source, ID,
exact-once cloze, answer equality, normalized target uniqueness across French 01–03, metadata, NFC,
ASCII-apostrophe, cloze-scope, French 04-boundary, coverage synchronization, and exactly three
representative-audio gates. A local checkpoint follows every five accepted slices.
