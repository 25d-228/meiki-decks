# French 04 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

- Locale and production model: `fr-FR`, contemporary metropolitan French at practical B2.
- Canonical learner input: NFC French spelling with standard accents and ASCII apostrophe U+0027.
- Planned size: exactly 2,000 original cards, `fr-04-0001` through `fr-04-2000`.
- Structure: fifty independent 40-card slices. The declared burdens close the practical B2 scope
  without adding cards merely to reach the planning ceiling.
- Immutable prerequisite: all 3,200 accepted French 01–03 cards and targets. A French 04 slice may
  use those cards and accepted earlier French 04 slices as support, never same-slice or later
  language.
- Default relationship model: `vous` for unfamiliar adults, public-facing and professional
  interaction; `tu` only where a close relationship is explicit.

Every card tests one focused, normalized-unique production span in a complete natural sentence or
short coherent exchange. A target does not hide a complete independent or dependent proposition,
manufacture uniqueness by arbitrary enlargement, or belong to a substitution or paradigm series.
Visible context remains meaningful and makes the answer fair.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Precise practical control of past narration, future anterior, conditional present and past,
  common counterfactual `si` patterns, and ordinary tense sequencing.
- Present and frequent past subjunctive with transparent indicative, infinitive, conditional, and
  subjunctive choice.
- Passive, impersonal, passive-like, causative, permissive, participial, gerund, infinitive,
  appositive, and reduced-clause constructions common in contemporary use.
- Natural direct and indirect pronouns, two-pronoun groups, `y`, `en`, and recoverable agreement.
- Productive relatives and frequent preposition-plus-relative patterns without gender or number
  tables.
- Cohesive cause, consequence, purpose, condition, concession, opposition, restriction,
  comparison, reformulation, attribution, qualification, and information packaging.

Literary tenses, rare mood sequences, dense pronoun chains, difficult agreement edge cases,
systematic compound-relative paradigms, and sustained C1 rhetoric remain outside this stage.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Narrate and explain familiar events, decisions, changes, complications, and consequences with
  controlled chronology and stance.
- Defend a position with reasons, examples, qualifications, and proportionate responses to
  objections; compare options and make calibrated recommendations.
- Summarize and discuss interviews, documentaries, articles, talks, reviews, and public
  information while separating source, report, interpretation, and opinion.
- Produce formal and semi-formal email, proposals, complaints, reports, reviews, follow-ups, and
  requests suited to the relationship.
- Manage workplace, study, project, deadline, meeting, presentation, travel, accommodation,
  housing, service, purchase, return, and dispute interaction independently.
- Interrupt, yield, clarify, reformulate, correct misunderstandings, and manage multi-turn
  interaction with appropriate directness.

## 3. Core lexical domains

- Personal and public events, decisions, change, evidence, consequences, uncertainty, and regret.
- Work, study, projects, meetings, deadlines, presentations, applications, and professional mail.
- Travel, transport, accommodation, housing, repairs, consumer services, purchases, and disputes.
- Culture, media, technology, education, environment, community, access, and familiar social
  questions.
- Practical health and access needs without diagnosis, treatment advice, or specialist claims.
- Source attribution, evidence, examples, reservation, comparison, recommendation, summary, and
  conclusion.

## 4. Collocations and reusable chunks

Coverage favors contemporary combinations such as `j'aurais dû`, `si j'avais su`, `d'ici là`,
`il se peut que`, `bien qu'il ait`, `faire réparer`, `se faire confirmer`, `ce à quoi`, `dans la
mesure où`, `quoi qu'il en soit`, `à en croire`, `il ressort que`, `cela dit`, `en partie`, `sous
réserve de`, and domain-specific chunks whose force is recoverable from context. Isolated synonym
lists, connector tables, document-field substitutions, and artificial argument templates do not
count.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- NFC, standard accents, ASCII apostrophe, canonical hyphens, capitalization, agreement, and
  punctuation; no IPA or phonetic respelling.
- Stable vowel and nasal-vowel contrasts, French `r`, elision, enchaînement, ordinary schwa,
  high-frequency liaison, and prohibited liaison.
- Connected auxiliary, pronoun, negative, relative, subordinate, passive, and discourse-marker
  groups with recoverable silent endings.
- Rhythmic grouping, final stress, contrastive focus, parenthetical phrasing, argument contour,
  reported-speech contour, and turn-management prosody.
- Spoken reductions may be listening targets, but typed production remains canonical French.

## 6. Register and relationship context

- `Vous` is binding for unfamiliar adults, staff, clients, public-facing roles, and professional
  contacts; `tu` requires an explicit close relationship.
- Formal and semi-formal messages use proportionate openings, requests, criticism, follow-up, and
  conclusions without bureaucratic imitation.
- Disagreement, refusal, complaint, correction, interruption, and advice are calibrated to role,
  evidence, and consequence.
- One exchange maintains stable relationship and speech level unless a change is explicit.
- Production is contemporary metropolitan French, excluding regional production, marked slang,
  caricature, literary archaism, and translation-like wording.

## 7. Pragmatics

- Claims are calibrated to the stated evidence; attribution distinguishes report from inference
  and opinion.
- Concessions, reservations, objections, recommendations, and criticism preserve face and remain
  proportionate.
- Pronouns and relatives have recoverable referents; chronology and reported speakers remain
  clear.
- Messages identify the actionable request, relevant constraint, and expected follow-up without
  hiding facts outside the cue.
- Reformulation and repair identify exactly what changes; summaries preserve source scope.

## 8. Listening targets

- Recover one focused canonical span from connected B2 narration, explanation, report, argument,
  summary, or interaction.
- Distinguish tense, mood, voice, polarity, pronoun order, agreement, source, stance, and referent.
- Track qualification, concession, cause, consequence, condition, restriction, reformulation, and
  topic continuity across multi-clause cues.
- Resolve connected groups, liaison, enchaînement, schwa, silent endings, contrastive focus, and
  parenthetical phrasing without phonetic support.
- Recover practical schedules, conditions, evidence, comparisons, constraints, and formal details.

## 9. Production targets

- Type one useful inflected form, collocation, pronoun group, voice or mood construction,
  connector, relative pattern, discourse marker, or compact interactional chunk.
- Select tense, mood, auxiliary, voice, agreement, pronoun position, reporting frame, preposition,
  stance marker, or connector from meaningful visible context.
- Produce practical narration, argument, summary, proposal, complaint, report, review, service,
  professional, and interaction-management language.
- Keep each target within one sentence, quotation, paragraph, and speaker turn, with terminal
  punctuation outside and enough context visible.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Every slice requires the 3,200 accepted French 01–03 cards and normalized target inventory.
- Slice 1 has no French 04 prerequisite; slices 2–50 require every accepted earlier French 04
  slice and its cumulative target inventory.
- Same-slice, later-slice, and French 05 language cannot supply hidden support.
- Learners can follow a connected B2 cue, identify stance and referents, and type one focused span
  without advanced linguistic terminology.

## 11. Exclusions

- Sustained synthesis of several long sources, extended academic writing, highly implicit or
  allusive argument, advanced stylistic analysis, and dense multi-speaker mediation.
- Literary or archaic grammar, rare mood sequences, specialist terminology without transparent
  context, diagnostic or treatment advice, policy scripts, and legal drafting.
- Pronoun-order tables, agreement tables, connector substitutions, gender/number/tense/noun/name/
  date/document-field series, dictionary blocks, and arbitrary target enlargement.
- Connector-plus-subject fragments, antecedent-plus-relative padding, whole propositions, long
  dictation, metadata leakage, filler, IPA, and phonetic respelling.
- French 05 cards or bundle work and any modification to French 01–03 or another language.

## Slice plan and binding burdens

### Slice 1 — `fr-04-0001`–`fr-04-0040`: precise connected past narration

- Production burden: coordinate completed events, background, interruption, and prior state through
  distinct natural narrative spans.
- Register: personal accounts, incident explanations, interviews, and professional updates.
- Pronunciation/spelling: auxiliary groups, imperfect endings, narrative rhythm, and agreement.
- Prerequisites: French 01–03 only.
- Excludes: literary tenses, long memoir passages, and tense-conjugation series.

### Slice 2 — `fr-04-0041`–`fr-04-0080`: chronology, revision, and consequences

- Production burden: revise an event sequence, locate turning points, and connect causes with later

- Pronunciation/spelling: temporal grouping, contrastive focus, liaison, and clause punctuation.
- Prerequisites: French 01–03 and S01.
- Excludes: unsupported hidden chronology, sensational testimony, and connector substitution.
