# French 05 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

- Locale and production model: `fr-FR`, contemporary metropolitan French at practical C1.
- Canonical learner input: NFC French spelling with standard accents and ASCII apostrophe U+0027.
- Planned size: exactly 2,200 original cards, `fr-05-0001` through `fr-05-2200`.
- Structure: fifty-five independent 40-card slices. Their declared burdens close the practical C1
  scope without filler or extension into French 06.
- Immutable prerequisite: all 5,200 accepted French 01–04 cards and normalized targets. A French
  05 slice may use those cards and accepted earlier French 05 slices as support, never same-slice,
  later-slice, or French 06 language.
- Default relationship model: `vous` for unfamiliar adults, public-facing roles, and professional
  contacts; `tu` only where a close relationship is explicit.

Every card tests one focused, normalized-unique span that directly realizes its slice burden in a
complete natural sentence or short coherent exchange. Visible context remains meaningful. Targets
do not hide complete propositions, manufacture uniqueness through arbitrary enlargement, or form
substitution, dictionary, or paradigm series.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Controlled narration and time reference across `passé composé`, `imparfait`,
  `plus-que-parfait`, future forms, conditional present, and conditional past.
- Real, hypothetical, and counterfactual relations whose time reference is recoverable.
- Natural indicative, present or past subjunctive, infinitive, conditional, gerund, participial,
  passive, impersonal, causative, permissive, pronominal, appositive, and reduced constructions.
- Recoverable direct and indirect pronouns, two-pronoun groups, `y`, `en`, agreement, productive
  relatives, common compound relatives, and `ce qui`, `ce que`, `ce dont`, and `ce à quoi`.
- Nominalization, dislocation, clefts, emphasis, topic continuity, referent control, reported
  perspective, and dense but readable ordinary information packaging.

Literary tenses, archaic mood forms, dense pronoun chains, systematic paradigms, and sustained C2
stylistic performance remain outside this stage.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Synthesize two or more explicitly stated positions, findings, or observations concisely and
  faithfully; compare compatible and conflicting claims and select relevant details.
- Attribute and paraphrase without invented quotations or hidden facts; separate evidence,
  report, inference, interpretation, and opinion.
- Build structured summaries, transitions, conclusions, and practical implications whose support
  is visible in the cue.
- Express nuanced agreement, reservation, disagreement, qualification, limitation,
  counterargument, probability, distance, and calibrated commitment.
- Criticize, correct, refuse, recommend, negotiate, mediate, interrupt, yield, clarify,
  reformulate, and repair with role-appropriate directness.
- Produce useful correspondence, reports, proposals, analyses, reviews, presentations,
  source-based explanations, follow-ups, and demanding practical interactions.

## 3. Core lexical domains

- Evidence, sources, findings, scope, uncertainty, interpretation, comparison, consequence,
  recommendation, implementation, accountability, and evaluation.
- Work, projects, meetings, negotiations, presentations, education, research communication,
  technology, media, and culture.
- Environment, community decisions, public services, access, travel, housing, consumer matters,
  and practical health or access needs without specialist claims.
- Formal, neutral, and close-peer language whose domain meaning is transparent from context.

## 4. Collocations and reusable chunks

Coverage favors contemporary combinations for attribution, qualification, synthesis, stance,
mediation, action, and evaluation, such as `il ressort de`, `à en juger par`, `rien ne permet de`,
`pour autant`, `sous réserve de`, `tenir compte de`, `mettre en perspective`, `aller dans le même
sens`, `revenir sur un point`, and `ce à quoi`. Each chunk carries a recoverable practical burden;
isolated inventories and arbitrary expansions do not count.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- NFC, standard accents, ASCII apostrophe, canonical hyphens, capitalization, agreement, and
  punctuation; no IPA or phonetic respelling.
- Connected auxiliaries, pronouns, negatives, relatives, subordinate groups, nominal groups, and
  discourse markers with natural liaison, enchaînement, schwa, and recoverable silent endings.
- Rhythmic grouping, contrastive focus, parenthetical phrasing, attribution contour, qualification,
  turn management, and dense but clear information packaging.
- Typed production remains canonical even when the audio cue contains ordinary spoken reduction.

## 6. Register and relationship context

- Formal and semi-formal professional, academic, public, and service language remains direct,
  contemporary, and proportionate rather than bureaucratic or ornate.
- Neutral cultural, media, community, consumer, housing, travel, and access language remains
  precise without unsupported authority claims.
- Close-peer interaction may use `tu` when the relationship is explicit; one interaction keeps a
  stable relationship and speech level unless a change is itself relevant.
- Production excludes regional imitation, marked slang, caricature, literary archaism, and
  translation-like wording.

## 7. Pragmatics

- Source scope, certainty, speaker stance, referents, and chronology remain explicit enough for a
  self-contained answer.
- Claims are calibrated to the evidence stated in the cue; no unseen article, interview, lecture,
  report, dataset, institution, quotation, policy, or statistic is assumed or fabricated.
- Disagreement, criticism, refusal, interruption, and recommendations preserve face and remain
  proportionate to role, evidence, and consequence.
- Reformulation identifies what changes; mediation represents each stated position fairly;
  conclusions follow from visible premises and lead to a practical implication when required.

## 8. Listening targets

- Recover one focused canonical span from connected C1 narration, source comparison, synthesis,
  argument, report, presentation, negotiation, mediation, or demanding practical interaction.
- Distinguish tense, mood, voice, pronoun order, agreement, attribution, source distance,
  qualification, concession, stance, and referent through meaningful context.
- Track two explicitly stated sources or positions without hidden knowledge, long rapid overlap,
  or unfamiliar regional accents.
- Resolve natural liaison, enchaînement, schwa, grouping, focus, parentheticals, and turn contours.

## 9. Production targets

- Type one useful inflected form, collocation, attribution frame, stance marker, pronoun group,
  relative pattern, information-structure construction, synthesis link, or interactional chunk.
- Make the target itself realize the declared slice burden; language outside the cloze cannot
  substitute for direct production.
- Preserve meaningful visible context, one sentence or speaker turn, and terminal punctuation
  outside the target.
- Produce original practical C1 language rather than templates, slogans, field substitutions, or
  long dictation.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Every slice requires the complete accepted French 01–04 source and normalized-target inventory.
- Slice 1 has no French 05 prerequisite; slices 2–55 require all accepted earlier French 05 slices
  and their cumulative target inventory.
- Same-slice, later-slice, and French 06 language cannot supply hidden support.
- Learners can follow a self-contained connected C1 cue and identify source, stance, chronology,
  referents, and the focused production burden without linguistic terminology.

## 11. Exclusions

- Several long or highly technical sources, sustained literary or rhetorical analysis, dense
  allusion, culturally dependent implication, extended irony or humor, and near-native claims.
- Long rapid multi-speaker overlap, systematic unfamiliar regional or social accents, and highly
  specialized terminology without transparent context.
- Literary grammar, rare mood sequences, grammar or pronoun tables, compound-relative paradigms,
  generic essay frames, debate slogans, and unsupported abstract claims.
- Gender, number, tense, noun, name, date, source, quotation, or document-field substitution
  series; dictionary blocks; connector-plus-subject fragments; relative-pronoun padding; arbitrary
  target enlargement; whole propositions; metadata leakage; IPA; and phonetic respelling.
- French 06 content, the French release bundle, changes to French 01–04, and changes to another
  language or the audio pipeline.
