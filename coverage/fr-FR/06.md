# French 06 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

- Locale and production model: `fr-FR`, contemporary metropolitan French at C2 and advanced use.
- Canonical learner input: NFC French spelling with standard accents and ASCII apostrophe U+0027.
- Planned size: exactly 2,600 original cards, `fr-06-0001` through `fr-06-2600`.
- Structure: sixty-five independent 40-card slices. This is the smallest permitted issue size and
  gives each required examination, source-handling, pragmatic, rhetorical, editing, interactional,
  domain, and language burden distinct coverage without filler.
- Immutable prerequisite: all 7,400 accepted French 01–05 cards and normalized targets. A French
  06 slice may use those cards and accepted earlier French 06 slices as support, never same-slice
  or later language.
- Default relationship model: `vous` for unfamiliar adults, public-facing roles, and professional
  contacts; `tu` only where a close relationship is explicit.

Every card tests one focused, normalized-unique span that directly realizes its slice burden in a
complete natural sentence or short coherent exchange. Visible context remains meaningful. Targets
do not hide complete propositions, manufacture uniqueness through arbitrary enlargement, or form
substitution, dictionary, label, or paradigm series.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Precise tense, aspect, chronology, perspective, mood, modality, hypothesis, and counterfactual
  relations in dense but recoverable contemporary use.
- Passive, impersonal, causative, permissive, pronominal, participial, gerund, nominal, appositive,
  reduced, and advanced subordinate constructions when they improve information structure.
- Recoverable pronouns, agreement, productive relatives, reference chains, dislocation, clefts,
  reported perspective, parentheticals, focus, and dense but readable packaging.
- Editing choices that repair ambiguity, cohesion, reference, register, concision, collocation,
  and rhetorical effect rather than display rare grammar.

Rare literary tenses, archaic mood forms, systematic paradigms, and opaque syntactic virtuosity are
excluded.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Reconstruct and concisely synthesize several explicitly stated positions, findings, or
  observations while preserving attribution, scope, source distance, and disagreement.
- Separate evidence, report, inference, assumption, interpretation, objection, counterexample,
  implication, recommendation, and proportionate conclusion.
- Qualify probability, limitation, responsibility, distance, and commitment precisely, then revise
  claims when new visible information warrants it.
- Recover and produce understatement, irony, humor, and indirect criticism only from sufficient
  local cues; analyze original short literary, stylistic, and rhetorical material in the cue.
- Mediate, negotiate, interrupt, yield, clarify, repair, and manage demanding multi-turn exchanges
  without hidden motives or unstable relationships.

## 3. Core lexical domains

- Sources, methods, findings, scope, uncertainty, attribution, evidence, causality, interpretation,
  assumptions, objections, implications, evaluation, and revision.
- Professional and academic work, research communication, education, technology, projects,
  negotiation, accountability, and institutional communication without invented institutions.
- Culture, media, literature, public discussion, community decisions, environment, services,
  consumer matters, housing, travel, access, and practical health needs without specialist claims.
- Rhetorical effect, textual organization, editing, register, interpersonal stance, and repair in
  transparent contemporary vocabulary.

## 4. Collocations and reusable chunks

Coverage favors precise contemporary combinations for reconstruction, attribution, synthesis,
qualification, argument, revision, mediation, editing, and evaluation. Examples include
`à ceci près que`, `sous cet angle`, `ne vaut que pour`, `faire la part de`, `à rebours de`,
`mettre en regard`, `à bien y regarder`, `quitte à`, `encore faut-il`, `ce qui revient à`, and
`sans pour autant`. Each target carries a recoverable practical burden; inventories, slogans,
arbitrary expansions, and recycled essay frames do not count.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- NFC, standard accents, ASCII apostrophe, canonical hyphens, capitalization, agreement, and
  punctuation; no IPA or phonetic respelling.
- Connected auxiliaries, clitics, negatives, relatives, subordinate groups, nominal groups, and
  discourse markers with natural liaison, enchaînement, schwa, and recoverable silent endings.
- Rhythmic grouping, contrastive focus, parenthetical phrasing, source attribution, qualification,
  interruption, yielding, irony, and turn contour through the fixed metropolitan-French voice.
- Typed production remains canonical even when the audio cue contains ordinary spoken reduction.

## 6. Register and relationship context

- Formal and semi-formal professional, academic, public, and service language remains direct,
  contemporary, and proportionate rather than bureaucratic, ornate, or falsely authoritative.
- Neutral cultural, media, community, consumer, housing, travel, access, and close-peer language
  remains precise, natural, and self-contained.
- One interaction keeps a stable relationship and speech level unless the change itself is the
  recoverable production burden.
- Regional or social forms appear only when explicitly attributed and locally understandable;
  production remains contemporary metropolitan French without accent imitation.

## 7. Pragmatics

- Source scope, certainty, speaker stance, referents, relationships, and chronology remain explicit
  enough for a self-contained answer.
- No card requires an unseen article, lecture, interview, dataset, institution, policy, quotation,
  statistic, literary work, specialist fact, or cultural allusion.
- Understatement, irony, humor, implication, and indirect criticism must have cues that make the
  intended force recoverable without guessing.
- Disagreement, criticism, interruption, refusal, and recommendations preserve face and remain
  proportionate to roles, evidence, and consequences.

## 8. Listening targets

- Recover one focused canonical span from dense but readable C2 narration, synthesis, analysis,
  negotiation, mediation, editing discussion, or source-based conclusion.
- Track several explicitly stated positions, qualifications, objections, and referents without
  hidden knowledge, long rapid overlap, or unfamiliar regional accents.
- Distinguish stance, scope, source distance, pragmatic force, grouping, focus, parentheticals,
  liaison, enchaînement, schwa, and turn contour through meaningful context.

## 9. Production targets

- Type one useful inflected form, collocation, attribution frame, stance marker, editing choice,
  synthesis link, rhetorical observation, source-management expression, or interactional chunk.
- Make the target itself realize the declared slice burden; language outside the cloze cannot
  substitute for direct production.
- Preserve meaningful visible context, one sentence or speaker turn, and terminal punctuation
  outside the target.
- Produce original advanced language rather than templates, slogans, labels, field substitutions,
  whole-proposition dictation, or near-native cultural performance.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Every slice requires the complete accepted French 01–05 source and normalized-target inventory.
- Slice 1 has no French 06 prerequisite; slices 2–65 require all accepted earlier French 06 slices
  and their cumulative target inventory.
- Same-slice and later-slice language cannot supply hidden support.
- Learners can follow a self-contained advanced cue and identify source, stance, chronology,
  referents, relationships, and a focused production burden without unseen specialist knowledge.

## 11. Exclusions

- Unseen sources, fabricated evidence or authority, unsupported quotations or statistics, hidden
  cultural knowledge, accent imitation, and claims of broad native competence.
- Literary archaism, rare grammar, opaque allusion, technical jargon without transparent context,
  long dictation, and long rapid multi-speaker overlap.
- Gender, number, tense, noun, name, date, source, quotation, rhetorical-device, or document-field
  substitution series; dictionary, paradigm, essay-frame, debate-slogan, and stylistic-label blocks.
- Connector-plus-subject fragments, antecedent-plus-relative padding, arbitrary target enlargement,
  whole propositions, unrelated adjunct targets, metadata leakage, and translated wording.
- French bundle configuration, release notes, final bundle-only tests, changes to French 01–05,
  Spanish work, another language, or the audio pipeline.
