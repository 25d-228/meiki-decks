# French 02 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

- Locale and production model: `fr-FR`, contemporary metropolitan French at practical A2.
- Canonical learner input: NFC French spelling with standard accents and ASCII apostrophe U+0027.
- Planned size: exactly 1,000 original cards, `fr-02-0001` through `fr-02-1000`.
- Structure: twenty-five independent 40-card slices. Completion depends on the burdens and
  contrasts below, not on reaching the last ID.
- Immutable prerequisite: all 800 accepted French 01 cards and targets. Each French 02 slice may
  use French 01 and accepted earlier French 02 slices as support, never same-slice or later language.
- Default relationship model: `vous` for unfamiliar adults, professional contacts, and service
  interactions; `tu` only with an explicitly identified friend, close peer, sibling, partner, or
  child.

Every card tests one focused, normalized-unique production span in a complete natural sentence or
short coherent exchange. Whole-sentence targets are limited to compact reusable fixed expressions.
Mechanical substitutions and long dictation do not count toward completion.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- `Passé composé` with `avoir` and `être`, common regular and irregular past participles, negation,
  questions, and required subject agreement with `être`.
- Introductory `imparfait` for age, clock time, weather, state, description, and repeated background
  activity; bounded event/background and completed-event/habit contrasts only.
- Recent past `venir de + infinitif`; chronology with `d'abord`, `ensuite`, `puis`, `après`,
  `avant de`, `pendant`, `depuis`, `il y a`, and related high-frequency chunks.
- Direct-object `le`, `la`, `les`; indirect-object `lui`, `leur`; common reflexive and reciprocal
  verbs; concrete place `y`; quantity and transparent `de`-complement `en`.
- Introductory pronoun placement before finite verbs and before infinitives in transparent
  two-verb constructions, including ordinary negative placement.
- Common affirmative and negative imperatives, including only the high-frequency pronoun placement
  required by practical A2 interaction; polite instructions remain softened where appropriate.
- `Il faut`, `devoir`, `pouvoir`, permission, prohibition, and ordinary obligation language.
- Practical future simple with regular verbs and essential irregular stems for arrangements,
  scheduled events, intentions, predictions, and likely outcomes; near future remains available.
- Partitives and quantity expressions, article changes after quantity and negation, and practical
  measurements, ranges, prices, dates, times, and frequencies.
- Comparatives and superlatives with adjectives, adverbs, nouns, and common verbs.
- Short useful relative clauses with `qui`, `que`, and `où`.
- Cause, consequence, purpose, sequence, contrast, and simple condition with high-frequency links;
  common prepositions for travel, location, duration, people, activities, and institutions.

Systematic literary narration, advanced tense sequencing, sustained tense discourse, difficult
past-participle agreement with preceding objects, complete multi-pronoun order, broad idiomatic
`y` or `en`, `dont`, compound relatives, and sustained argumentation are reserved for French 03.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Recount recent and past events, trips, appointments, disruptions, problems, and experiences.
- Describe childhood, earlier circumstances, recurring past activity, and event background at
  introductory depth.
- Ask for and give instructions, obligation, permission, prohibition, recommendations, reasons,
  comparisons, choices, and simple opinions.
- Arrange, invite, cancel, reschedule, predict, and explain a change of plan.
- Handle accommodation, transport, directions, schedules, delays, and travel service problems.
- Handle returns, exchanges, quantities, payment, ordinary complaints, restaurant problems,
  simple recipe descriptions, and practical requests.
- Coordinate home repairs, neighborhood needs, lost property, work, study, training, colleagues,
  and ordinary tasks.
- State immediate non-specialist health needs without diagnosis or medical advice.
- Discuss leisure, culture, media, holidays, and familiar personal experiences.
- Clarify, correct, apologize, confirm, request repair, and maintain a stable relationship level.

## 3. Core lexical domains

- Everyday events, memories, childhood, chronology, weather, feelings, circumstances, and habits.
- Travel, accommodation, stations, tickets, routes, schedules, delays, cancellations, luggage, and
  directions.
- Appointments, invitations, calendars, changes of plan, and practical future arrangements.
- Shops, products, sizes, stock, receipts, returns, exchanges, quantities, prices, and payment.
- Restaurants, ingredients, quantities, simple preparation descriptions, preferences, and service
  problems.
- Homes, neighborhoods, appliances, repairs, maintenance, lost property, and practical help.
- Workplaces, courses, training, colleagues, tasks, deadlines, and routine coordination.
- Body and comfort, minor symptoms, pharmacies, appointments, and immediate non-specialist needs.
- Leisure, sport, culture, media, holidays, outings, and familiar experiences.

## 4. Collocations and reusable chunks

Coverage favors ordinary combinations such as `avoir oublié`, `être arrivé en retard`,
`quand j'étais petit`, `venir de finir`, `rendre un article`, `faire réparer`, `prendre
rendez-vous`, `être en panne`, `avoir besoin de`, `demander à quelqu'un`, `penser à`, `s'occuper
de`, `aller y`, `en prendre deux`, `plus ... que`, `le lieu où`, `pour + infinitif`, and
interactional chunks whose force is clear from context. Dictionary
series, conjugation tables, and translated textbook prose do not count.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- Standard accented NFC spelling; no IPA or phonetic respelling in learner-facing fields.
- ASCII apostrophe U+0027 in every committed French sentence, target, answer, and support field.
- Stable vowel distinctions, principal nasal-vowel contrasts, French `r`, and ordinary schwa
  presence or absence where recognition changes but spelling does not.
- Elision, enchaînement, high-frequency liaison, and prohibited liaison in natural connected cues.
- Connected auxiliary, pronoun, and reflexive groups; recoverable past-participle endings and common
  homophones; future and imperfect endings supported by context.
- Rhythmic groups, final stress, question contour, instruction contour, and connected narrative
  delivery. Accents, agreement, apostrophes, hyphens, and punctuation remain canonical.

## 6. Register and relationship context

- `Vous` is binding for unfamiliar adults, staff, professional contacts, and service encounters.
- `Tu` is binding only where a close relationship is explicit in the sentence, exchange, or concise
  `register` support.
- One interaction never switches between `tu` and `vous` without an explicit relationship change.
- Commands are softened when role or situation requires it; workplace coordination identifies
  authority and ownership without bureaucratic prose.
- Use ordinary contemporary metropolitan French, not regional production, marked slang, literary
  archaism, role caricature, or translation-like wording.

## 7. Pragmatics

- Narration makes event order and background clear without requiring unstated facts.
- Requests, instructions, refusals, complaints, apologies, and recommendations match the setting
  and relationship.
- Service problems state the concrete problem and requested remedy without escalation scripts.
- Comparisons and opinions remain calibrated, simple, and grounded in visible context.
- Pronouns have one recoverable antecedent; `y` and `en` have a concrete transparent source.
- Repair language identifies the uncertain or corrected detail.

## 8. Listening targets

- Recover one focused canonical span from a complete natural cue or short coherent exchange.
- Distinguish auxiliary, pronoun, polarity, tense, agreement, chronology, and question force.
- Resolve connected pronoun/auxiliary groups, elision, liaison, enchaînement, schwa, and silent
  endings from context without phonetic respelling.
- Recover quantities, prices, dates, times, measurements, ranges, routes, and comparison terms.
- Track referents and event order across one short connected exchange or narrative cue.

## 9. Production targets

- Type one useful inflected form, pronoun group, collocation, or compact construction in canonical
  spelling.
- Select tense, auxiliary, agreement, pronoun position, imperative form, article, comparison,
  relative, preposition, or connector from the visible situation.
- Produce practical service, repair, narration, instruction, and coordination language while
  maintaining relationship and speech level.
- Keep every target within one sentence, quotation, paragraph, and speaker turn, with terminal
  punctuation outside the target.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Every slice requires all 800 accepted French 01 cards and their primary-target inventory.
- Slice 1 has no French 02 prerequisite; slices 2–25 require every accepted earlier French 02 slice
  and its cumulative target inventory.
- Same-slice and later-slice French 02 language cannot provide hidden support.
- Learners can hear one complete short cue and type one focused span; no IPA or advanced grammar
  terminology is assumed.

## 11. Exclusions

- Systematic literary narration, advanced tense sequencing, sustained passé composé–imparfait
  discourse control, pluperfect, past historic, and advanced conditional or subjunctive systems.
- Complete multi-pronoun order, difficult preceding-object agreement, broad idiomatic `y`/`en`,
  `dont`, compound relatives, advanced comparison, and sustained argumentation.
- Specialist documents, diagnosis, medical advice, legal/financial guidance, formal administrative
  prose, and extended workplace reporting.
- Regional production, marked slang, literary archaism, phonetic respelling, IPA answers,
  dictionary series, translation drills, mechanical substitutions, and internal process language.
- French 03 cards or source, release-bundle work, and changes to French 01.

## Slice plan and binding burdens

### Slice 1 — `fr-02-0001`–`fr-02-0040`: completed everyday events with `avoir`

- Production burden: affirmative `passé composé` with `avoir`; high-frequency regular participles
  and bounded events in home, work, study, shopping, and leisure contexts.
- Register: neutral narration and explicit close-peer or polite information exchange.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected subject–auxiliary groups, `é` participles, elision, and rhythmic
  grouping.
- Prerequisites: French 01 only.
- Excludes: irregular-participle series, object pronouns, event/background contrast, and long story.

### Slice 2 — `fr-02-0041`–`fr-02-0080`: irregular participles, negation, and past questions

- Production burden: common irregular participles with `avoir`; negative completed events;
  information and yes/no questions about ordinary past actions.
- Register: polite questions for unfamiliar contacts; relationship-supported `tu` elsewhere.
- Pronunciation/spelling: auxiliary liaison where natural, silent participle endings, accents, and
  past-question contour.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01.
- Excludes: conjugation tables, preceding-object agreement, and literary inversion.

### Slice 3 — `fr-02-0081`–`fr-02-0120`: movement events with `être`

- Production burden: common `être`-auxiliary movement verbs, subject gender/number agreement,
  negation, questions, arrivals, departures, returns, and short travel events.
- Register: personal narration and polite travel/accommodation interaction.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected auxiliary groups and audible/recoverable agreement endings.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S02.
- Excludes: reflexive past, passive voice, exhaustive auxiliary classification, and long narration.

### Slice 4 — `fr-02-0121`–`fr-02-0160`: introductory `imparfait` states and description

- Production burden: age, time, weather, physical or emotional state, place description, and
  background circumstances in the `imparfait`.
- Register: neutral memories and relationship-supported personal questions.
- Pronunciation/spelling: imperfect endings, homophone recovery from context, and narrative rhythm.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S03.
- Excludes: systematic tense contrast, literary description, and hypothetical conditional force.

### Slice 5 — `fr-02-0161`–`fr-02-0200`: earlier habits and bounded tense contrast

- Production burden: repeated past activity plus a bounded completed event; `quand`, `souvent`,
  `d'habitude`, and concise passé composé–imparfait contrasts.
- Register: childhood, family, close-peer, and neutral autobiographical contexts.
- Pronunciation/spelling: tense endings, connected clauses, and phrase-final grouping.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S04.
- Excludes: sustained discourse control, repeated mechanical tense pairs, and advanced sequencing.

### Slice 6 — `fr-02-0201`–`fr-02-0240`: recent past and chronology

- Production burden: `venir de + infinitif`, `d'abord`, `ensuite`, `puis`, `après`, `avant de`,
  `pendant`, `depuis`, and `il y a` in practical event order and duration.
- Register: ordinary personal and service updates.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected `venir de`, elision, liaison, and chronology rhythm groups.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S05.
- Excludes: advanced temporal clauses, pluperfect, and same-slice-only hidden support.

### Slice 7 — `fr-02-0241`–`fr-02-0280`: short connected A2 narration

- Production burden: two- or three-clause accounts of trips, appointments, ordinary problems, and
  familiar experiences with explicit chronology and one focused target.
- Register: neutral recounting, close-peer experience sharing, and polite problem reporting.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected narrative delivery, enchaînement, liaison, and final stress.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S06.
- Excludes: hidden paragraph context, sustained tense discourse, argumentation, and long dictation.

### Slice 8 — `fr-02-0281`–`fr-02-0320`: direct objects before finite verbs

- Production burden: `le`, `la`, and `les` with clear antecedents in affirmative and negative
  present contexts; ordinary preferences, tasks, media, belongings, and transactions.
- Register: neutral, close-peer, professional, and polite service contexts.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected pronoun–verb groups, elision, and agreement outside the target.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S07.
- Excludes: double pronouns, preceding-object participle agreement, and ambiguous antecedents.

### Slice 9 — `fr-02-0321`–`fr-02-0360`: direct objects with infinitives and past events

- Production burden: direct pronouns before infinitives in transparent two-verb constructions and

- Register: practical plans, service follow-up, and personal updates.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected pronoun–auxiliary/infinitive groups and negation placement.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S08.
- Excludes: difficult participle agreement, multi-pronoun chains, and opaque idioms.

### Slice 10 — `fr-02-0361`–`fr-02-0400`: indirect objects `lui` and `leur`

- Production burden: common person-recipient contexts in present, near future, and transparent past

- Register: clear family/friend relationships and polite professional roles.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected indirect-pronoun groups and future/auxiliary rhythm.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S09.
- Excludes: double-object order, ethical datives, and ambiguous recipients.

### Slice 11 — `fr-02-0401`–`fr-02-0440`: reflexive and reciprocal events

- Production burden: common reflexive routines, changes of state, practical events, and reciprocal

- Register: family, partners, close peers, and neutral third-person description.
- Pronunciation/spelling: elision, connected reflexive groups, and `être` agreement when required.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S10.
- Excludes: reflexive paradigms, lexicalized advanced idioms, and reciprocal ambiguity.

### Slice 12 — `fr-02-0441`–`fr-02-0480`: concrete place pronoun `y`

- Production burden: recoverable destinations and locations with `y` before finite verbs and before

- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S11.
- Excludes: idiomatic `y`, double pronouns, and abstract complements.

### Slice 13 — `fr-02-0481`–`fr-02-0520`: quantity and transparent complement `en`

- Production burden: recoverable quantities and `de` complements with `en`, explicit amount
  retention, negation, and transparent infinitive placement.
- Register: shopping, food, home, travel, and ordinary preference contexts.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected `j'en`, `on en`, liaison, and quantity grouping.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S12.
- Excludes: idiomatic `en`, double pronouns, vague antecedents, and advanced partitive analysis.

### Slice 14 — `fr-02-0521`–`fr-02-0560`: ordinary imperatives and placement

- Production burden: common affirmative and negative imperatives, practical directions,
  instructions, safety, and high-frequency pronoun placement required by the interaction.
- Register: explicit close relationships for `tu`; softened `vous` instructions in service or

- Pronunciation/spelling: instruction contour, imperative hyphens, elision, and rhythmic grouping.
- Prerequisites: French 01 and S01–S13.
- Excludes: paradigm drills, stacked pronouns, bare commands to unfamiliar adults, and formal rules.

### Slice 15 — `fr-02-0561`–`fr-02-0600`: obligation, permission, and prohibition

- Production burden: `il faut`, `devoir`, `pouvoir`, permission requests, ordinary prohibitions,

### Slice 16 — `fr-02-0601`–`fr-02-0640`: practical future simple

For every slice, a fresh author process returns exactly the assigned 40 cards from the binding
matrix and cumulative accepted-target index. A separate fresh metropolitan-French editor returns a
fully corrected 40-card JSON batch. The executor accepts only a batch that passes source, ID,
exact-once cloze, answer equality, normalized target uniqueness across French 01–02, metadata, NFC,
ASCII-apostrophe, French 03-boundary, coverage synchronization, and representative-audio gates.
