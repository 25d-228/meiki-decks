# French 01 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

- Locale and production model: `fr-FR`, contemporary metropolitan French.
- Canonical learner input: standard French spelling with accents and the ASCII apostrophe U+0027.
- Planned size: exactly 800 original cards, `fr-01-0001` through `fr-01-0800`.
- Structure: twenty independent 40-card slices. Completion depends on the coverage and contrasts
  below, not on reaching the last ID.
- Default relationship model: `vous` with unfamiliar adults, professional contacts, and service
  staff; `tu` only with an explicitly identified friend, close peer, sibling, partner, or child.
- Entry point: no French prerequisite beyond the ability to hear a short cue and type accented
  Latin text. Each later slice requires the accepted source from all earlier slices.

Every card tests one focused, normalized-unique production span in a complete natural sentence or
short coherent exchange. Tense, number, gender, name, or price substitution alone does not create
new coverage. Whole-sentence targets are limited to compact reusable fixed expressions.

## 1. Grammar and constructions

- Definite, indefinite, and high-frequency partitive articles, including elision and ordinary
  article choice after negation where A1 use requires it.
- Noun gender and number; common adjective agreement and common pre- and post-nominal position.
- Subject pronouns, natural `on`, and basic stressed pronouns in contrast or after prepositions.
- Present tense of productive regular verbs and the essential irregular verbs `être`, `avoir`,
  `aller`, `faire`, `venir`, `pouvoir`, `vouloir`, `devoir`, `prendre`, plus high-frequency A1
  verbs required by the domains below.
- `c'est`, `ce sont`, `il est`, `elle est`, and `il y a` with clear identification, description,
  and existence functions.
- Ordinary `ne ... pas` negation, with recognition of normal spoken omission of `ne` only when the
  complete context makes the intended negative unambiguous.
- Intonation questions, `est-ce que`, question words, and only fixed high-frequency polite
  inversion such as `Avez-vous ... ?`.
- Possessive and demonstrative adjectives; high-frequency place and time prepositions.
- Near future `aller + infinitive` for concrete plans.
- Quantities, prices, dates, clock time, age, telephone numbers, and practical measurements.
- High-frequency coordination and short links with `et`, `mais`, `ou`, `parce que`, `donc`,
  `puis`, and `alors` where they serve a practical A1 interaction.

Productive past narration, direct and indirect object-pronoun systems, reflexive paradigms,
productive imperative paradigms, `y`, `en`, and broad relative clauses are reserved for French 02.

## 2. Communicative functions

- Exchange identity, nationality, language, work, study, and contact information.
- Describe family, ordinary relationships, homes, rooms, common objects, possession, and location.
- State routines, frequency, opening times, dates, appointments, schedules, and near-term plans.
- Order food and drink; state preferences; request quantities; check availability and prices.
- Shop for size, color, quantity, price, and payment in ordinary service interactions.
- Use transport, identify destinations, ask directions, and state routes and travel time.
- Describe simple weather and seasonal activity.
- State immediate basic needs and simple non-specialist problems.
- Greet, introduce, thank, apologize, clarify, confirm, correct, request repetition, and repair.
- Invite, accept, decline tactfully, express preferences, choose, request, and complete routine
  transactions.

## 3. Core lexical domains

- Personal information, countries, nationalities, languages, occupations, workplaces, schools,
  courses, telephone numbers, and email spelling.
- Family and ordinary relationships, age, simple appearance, and personality descriptions.
- Homes, rooms, furniture, appliances, belongings, possession, location, and nearby places.
- Daily routine, recurring activities, frequency, weekdays, and ordinary leisure.
- Food, drink, meals, cafés, restaurants, menus, quantities, tastes, and preferences.
- Shops, products, clothing, colors, sizes, money, prices, payment, and stock.
- Streets, stations, stops, vehicles, tickets, destinations, directions, and travel time.
- Calendar, clock time, appointments, duration, schedules, invitations, and plans.
- Weather, seasons, temperature, outings, and simple seasonal choices.
- Body and comfort, hunger, thirst, tiredness, minor symptoms, lost items, delays, and help.

## 4. Collocations and reusable chunks

Coverage favors ordinary combinations such as `parler français`, `travailler à Paris`,
`prendre le métro`, `avoir rendez-vous`, `boire un café`, `payer par carte`, `faire les courses`,
`aller au travail`, `venir en train`, `avoir besoin de`, and interactional chunks whose force is
clear from the relationship and situation. Dictionary-entry series and mechanical substitutions do
not count as independent coverage.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- Standard accented spelling and NFC text; no IPA or phonetic respelling in answers or metadata.
- ASCII apostrophe U+0027 in every committed French sentence, target, answer, and support field.
- Recycled `/u/`–`/y/`, `/e/`–`/ɛ/`, useful `/ø/`–`/œ/`, and principal nasal-vowel contrasts in
  high-frequency words whose meaning is recoverable from the complete cue.
- French `r`, silent final consonants, and common pronounced-final exceptions in ordinary words.
- Elision, high-frequency liaison, prohibited-liaison awareness, and enchaînement through natural
  phrases rather than pronunciation labels.
- Rhythmic groups, phrase-final stress, statement contour, and question contour in short connected
  delivery. Punctuation stays outside the typed target.

## 6. Register and relationship context

- `vous` is binding for unfamiliar adults, staff, professional contacts, and service encounters.
- `tu` is binding only where a close relationship is explicit in the sentence, exchange, or concise
  `register` support.
- A single interaction never switches between `tu` and `vous` without an explicit relationship
  change.
- Use ordinary contemporary metropolitan French, not marked slang, literary archaism, role speech,
  or regional production.

## 7. Pragmatics

- Greetings, thanks, apologies, and leave-taking match the situation and time of day.
- Requests and invitations are appropriately softened; refusals remain clear but non-hostile.
- Confirmation, correction, clarification, and repetition requests identify the uncertain detail.
- Service exchanges give the staff member and customer natural roles and do not use `tu`.
- Personal questions remain plausible for the stated relationship and setting.

## 8. Listening targets

- Recover one focused canonical span from a complete natural cue.
- Distinguish article, gender, number, agreement, present-tense form, polarity, and question force.
- Resolve numbers, dates, clock time, prices, quantities, routes, and telephone digits from context.
- Hear elision, liaison, enchaînement, rhythmic grouping, and silent-final behavior without changing
  canonical spelling.
- Track the same participant across a short two-turn exchange without requiring a hidden paragraph.

## 9. Production targets

- Type one useful word, collocation, inflected form, or compact construction in standard spelling.
- Select article, agreement, verb form, negation, question form, preposition, or connector from the
  situation rather than an English translation prompt.
- Maintain `tu`/`vous` consistently and produce conventional service and repair language.
- Produce practical numbers and measurements in the form required by the sentence.
- Keep accents, apostrophes, spaces, and punctuation boundaries accurate.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Slice 1 has no French-language prerequisite.
- Slices 2–20 require all earlier accepted slices and their cumulative primary-target index.
- Learners must be able to listen to one complete short cue and type a focused hidden span.
- No prior grammatical terminology, IPA, or CEFR preparation is assumed.

## 11. Exclusions

- Productive past narration, object-pronoun order, reflexive paradigms, broad imperative production,
  `y`, `en`, and broader relative clauses.
- Subjunctive, conditional, future simple, passive, literary tenses, advanced comparison, and formal
  administrative or workplace writing.
- Regional French production, marked slang, literary archaism, phonetic respelling, IPA answers,
  translation drills, conjugation tables, and isolated pronunciation labels.
- Mechanical name, number, gender, tense, or noun substitution; long sentence dictation; dictionary
  series; internal generation or review language in learner-facing fields.

## Slice plan and binding burdens

### Slice 1 — `fr-01-0001`–`fr-01-0040`: greetings, identity, and `être`

- Production burden: greetings and leave-taking; names, origins, languages, and occupations;
  subject pronouns; `être`; `c'est`; definite and indefinite singular articles; basic statements,
  polarity, and intonation questions.
- Register: first meetings use `vous`; `tu` appears only between explicitly named close peers.
- Pronunciation/spelling: accents in `être`, `français`, and nationalities; elision in `c'est` and
  `j'habite`; statement versus question contour.
- Prerequisites: none beyond stage entry.
- Excludes: family detail, possession, plural systems, and extended biography.

### Slice 2 — `fr-01-0041`–`fr-01-0080`: nationality, language, work, and study

- Production burden: nationality and adjective agreement; languages; `parler`, `habiter`,
  `travailler`, and `étudier`; `il est`/`elle est` versus `c'est`; workplace and school prepositions.
- Register: polite information exchange with a new classmate, colleague, or receptionist.
- Pronunciation/spelling: masculine/feminine written endings, `/e/`–`/ɛ/`, and silent finals.
- Prerequisites: slice 1.
- Excludes: past study history, formal résumés, and broad profession-gender debates.

### Slice 3 — `fr-01-0081`–`fr-01-0120`: family, relationships, and possession

- Production burden: family nouns with gender; plural number; possessive adjectives; `avoir`;
  age; basic stressed pronouns; simple descriptions with common adjective position.
- Register: `tu` only within explicit family or close-friend exchanges; third-person description
  remains neutral and respectful.
- Pronunciation/spelling: liaison in common plural noun phrases, silent plural endings, nasal vowels,
  and accent retention.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–2.
- Excludes: object pronouns, reflexive relationships, and family narratives.

### Slice 4 — `fr-01-0121`–`fr-01-0160`: home, objects, existence, and location

- Production burden: rooms and common objects; `il y a`; demonstrative adjectives; location with
  `dans`, `sur`, `sous`, `devant`, `derrière`, `à côté de`, and `chez`; possession and availability.
- Register: neutral household description and polite housing questions.
- Pronunciation/spelling: elision, final consonants in spatial chunks, `/u/`–`/y/`, and `où` accent.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–3.
- Excludes: relative clauses, home-repair instructions, and property contracts.

### Slice 5 — `fr-01-0161`–`fr-01-0200`: routine and regular present tense

- Production burden: high-frequency regular `-er` actions; daily routine; frequency adverbs;
  ordinary subject omission avoidance; `ne ... pas`; `et`, `puis`, and `mais` in short links.
- Register: neutral self-description; close-peer questions may use explicit `tu` context.
- Pronunciation/spelling: silent verb endings, rhythmic groups, elision in negation, and canonical
  written `ne` despite recognition of common spoken omission.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–4.
- Excludes: productive reflexive routine verbs, past narration, and conjugation-table cards.

### Slice 6 — `fr-01-0201`–`fr-01-0240`: essential irregular present forms

- Production burden: useful present forms of `aller`, `faire`, `venir`, `prendre`, `pouvoir`,
  `vouloir`, and `devoir` in destinations, activities, ability, preference, and obligation.
- Register: ordinary polite questions and answers; no bare commands.
- Pronunciation/spelling: vowel alternations, silent endings, liaison in high-frequency fixed groups,
  and French `r`.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–5.
- Excludes: full paradigms without communicative purpose, imperative forms, and conditional requests.

### Slice 7 — `fr-01-0241`–`fr-01-0280`: questions, clarification, and repair

- Production burden: intonation questions, `est-ce que`, `qui`, `que`/`qu'est-ce que`, `où`,
  `quand`, `comment`, `combien`, `pourquoi`; fixed polite inversion; repetition, spelling,
  confirmation, and correction chunks.
- Register: `vous` for service and unfamiliar contacts; relationship-supported `tu` elsewhere.
- Pronunciation/spelling: question contour, elision, accents in question words, and phrase grouping.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–6.
- Excludes: broad inversion drills, embedded questions, and indirect-question syntax.

### Slice 8 — `fr-01-0281`–`fr-01-0320`: numbers and personal details

- Production burden: age, telephone numbers, addresses, room and floor numbers, prices, simple
  quantities, and practical measurements; `combien`; `avoir` in age and contact contexts.
- Register: polite exchange of necessary personal or transaction details.
- Pronunciation/spelling: number linking, `six`/`dix` final behavior in context, liaison before nouns,
  and unambiguous grouping of digit strings.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–7.
- Excludes: arithmetic drills, number-only cues, and mechanical substitutions.

### Slice 9 — `fr-01-0321`–`fr-01-0360`: dates, time, schedules, and appointments

- Production burden: weekdays, months, dates, clock time, `à`, `de ... à`, `avant`, `après`,
  appointment setting, opening times, duration, and frequency.
- Register: polite scheduling with staff or a new contact; close-friend planning is explicit.
- Pronunciation/spelling: hour boundaries, liaison, rhythmic grouping, accents, and unambiguous
  distinction between date and duration.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–8.
- Excludes: past calendars, future simple, and dense timetable dictation.

### Slice 10 — `fr-01-0361`–`fr-01-0400`: food, drink, articles, and preferences

- Production burden: meals, common food and drink, definite/indefinite/partitive choice, quantity
  chunks, `aimer`, `préférer`, `manger`, `boire`, and negation of ordinary consumption.
- Register: home and friendly preferences plus neutral questions from a host.
- Pronunciation/spelling: partitive contractions, nasal vowels, `/ø/`–`/œ/`, elision, and accents.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–9.
- Excludes: recipes, imperative cooking instructions, and exhaustive food lists.

### Slice 11 — `fr-01-0401`–`fr-01-0440`: café and restaurant service

- Production burden: greeting staff, requesting a table, ordering, quantities, availability,
  choosing, changing or confirming a simple order, asking for the bill, and paying.
- Register: binding customer–staff `vous`; concise conventional politeness throughout.
- Pronunciation/spelling: service rhythmic groups, enchaînement, polite question contour, and
  canonical contractions.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–10.
- Excludes: `tu`, marked waiter stereotypes, complex complaints, and conditional mood production.

### Slice 12 — `fr-01-0441`–`fr-01-0480`: shopping, color, size, price, and payment

- Production burden: demonstratives, adjective position/agreement, clothing and daily goods,
  availability, sizes, colors, quantities, prices, choice, cash/card payment, and receipts.
- Register: binding customer–staff `vous` with natural role ownership.
- Pronunciation/spelling: adjective endings, silent plurals, color spelling, liaison, and prices.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–11.
- Excludes: object pronouns, comparative paradigms, returns policy, and bargaining scripts.

### Slice 13 — `fr-01-0481`–`fr-01-0520`: transport and destinations

- Production burden: `aller`, `venir`, `prendre`; `à`, `au`, `aux`, `en`; buses, trains, metro,
  stations, stops, tickets, destinations, route numbers, departure, arrival, and travel time.
- Register: polite passenger–staff and stranger questions; friend travel plans are explicit.
- Pronunciation/spelling: contractions, liaison, enchaînement, French `r`, and number grouping.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–12.
- Excludes: object pronouns, past trips, and detailed disruption announcements.

### Slice 14 — `fr-01-0521`–`fr-01-0560`: directions and nearby places

- Production burden: common city places; asking and giving simple location information with
  `à gauche`, `à droite`, `tout droit`, `près de`, `loin de`, `entre`, `en face de`; simple distance.
- Register: `vous` between strangers; no bare imperative system.
- Pronunciation/spelling: liaison and enchaînement in location chunks, accents, and phrase-final
  contour that separates route steps.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–13.
- Excludes: productive imperatives, complex routes, and spatial narratives.

### Slice 15 — `fr-01-0561`–`fr-01-0600`: invitations, choices, and near-future plans

- Production burden: `aller + infinitive`, concrete plans, invitations, accepting, tactful refusal,
  alternatives with `ou`, preferences, meeting place/time, and short reasons with `parce que`.
- Register: clear friend/close-peer `tu` contexts and separate polite `vous` invitations.
- Pronunciation/spelling: connected `aller + infinitive`, question contour, rhythmic groups, and
  canonical apostrophes.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–14.
- Excludes: future simple, conditional invitations, and extended excuses.

### Slice 16 — `fr-01-0601`–`fr-01-0640`: weather, seasons, and simple activity

- Production burden: `il fait`, `il y a`, weather adjectives/nouns, seasons, temperature, suitable
  clothing, and near-term activity choices linked with `donc`, `mais`, or `parce que`.
- Register: neutral small talk and relationship-appropriate planning.
- Pronunciation/spelling: nasal-vowel contrasts, silent finals, weather liaison, and accents.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–15.
- Excludes: forecasts with future simple, climate debate, and advanced impersonal constructions.

### Slice 17 — `fr-01-0641`–`fr-01-0680`: immediate needs and simple problems

- Production burden: hunger, thirst, fatigue, temperature comfort, basic symptoms, lost items,
  delays, closures, inability, obligation, `avoir besoin de`, requesting help, and simple responses.
- Register: polite help requests to unfamiliar adults; direct wording only for immediate safety.
- Pronunciation/spelling: liaison in `besoin de`, nasal vowels, French `r`, and clear negative scope.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–16.
- Excludes: diagnosis, medical instructions, emergency procedures, and object-pronoun systems.

### Slice 18 — `fr-01-0681`–`fr-01-0720`: social courtesy and interaction management

- Production burden: time-appropriate greetings, introductions, thanks, apologies, leave-taking,
  attention, confirmation, misunderstanding, repetition, slower speech, spelling, and simple repair.
- Register: systematic contrast between service/professional `vous` and explicit close-peer `tu`.
- Pronunciation/spelling: elision, prohibited liaison awareness in natural contexts, question versus
  statement contour, and compact fixed expressions only.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–17.
- Excludes: marked slang, elaborate apologies, and metalinguistic grammar explanations.

### Slice 19 — `fr-01-0721`–`fr-01-0760`: connected A1 transactions

- Production burden: short two-turn exchanges integrating identity/contact detail, scheduling,
  food, shopping, transport, or directions; one focused target per exchange; practical connectors.
- Register: role and relationship stay explicit and stable across each exchange.
- Pronunciation/spelling: liaison, enchaînement, rhythmic grouping, number grouping, elision, and
  canonical recovery from connected delivery.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–18.
- Excludes: long dialogues, hidden multi-turn passages, French 02 grammar, and review duplicates.

### Slice 20 — `fr-01-0761`–`fr-01-0800`: complete A1 gap closure

- Production burden: close only verified gaps across articles, agreement, essential present forms,
  questions, negation, prepositions, quantities, time, plans, service language, and pragmatic repair;
  every target must add a distinct useful production burden.
- Register: cumulative audit of `tu`/`vous`, professional/service roles, family, and close peers.
- Pronunciation/spelling: cumulative accents, apostrophes, silent finals, liaison, enchaînement,
  rhythmic grouping, and terminal-punctuation boundaries.
- Prerequisites: slices 1–19 and their complete accepted-target index.
- Excludes: filler, mechanical contrast pairs, long dictation, French 02 grammar, and any target
  already normalized-equivalent to an accepted French 01 target.

## Slice acceptance workflow

Each slice uses one fresh no-context author and a separate fresh metropolitan-French editor. Both
receive the complete issue contract, the exact slice, its ID range, and the cumulative accepted
primary-target index. After editorial acceptance, the source and this matrix are synchronized, all
stage gates run, and representative audio is objectively validated before the next slice starts.
