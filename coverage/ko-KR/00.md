# Korean 00 coverage matrix

## Stage purpose

- Locale and variety: `ko-KR`, contemporary standard South Korean.
- Canonical learner input: Hangul only. Romanization and phonetic respelling are prohibited.
- Planned size: exactly 300 cards, `ko-00-0001` through `ko-00-0300`.
- Structure: ten fresh-context slices of 30 cards. The slices are generation and review units, but their Korean content is cumulative according to the prerequisite graph below.
- Outcome: direct reading and typing of beginner Hangul; control of the major consonant and vowel contrasts; recognition and production of common 받침 and connected-speech behavior; phrase rhythm and final intonation; and 40 essential polite expressions.
- Boundary: this is a script-and-sound foundation with compact fixed language, not a grammar survey or a compressed proficiency course.

Every generated card must have one focused production target. A sound-focused card must use a complete, meaningful utterance and enough visible or semantic context to make the standard spelling recoverable. No card may require an underlying spelling to be guessed from a neutralized final sound alone.

## 4. Collocations and chunks

- Greeting sequence: `안녕하세요` → `처음 뵙겠습니다` → `만나서 반가워요` → `잘 부탁드립니다` (slices 1 and 7). Only the four expressions themselves are targets; the sequence is listening and role-play support.
- Leave-taking pair: `안녕히 가세요` for a person who is leaving and `안녕히 계세요` for a person who is staying (slice 2).
- Understanding repair: `잘 모르겠어요`, `이해가 안 돼요`, `알겠어요`, `다시 말해 주세요` (slice 5).
- Pace and assistance: `천천히 말해 주세요`, `도와주세요`, `잠깐만요`, `여기요` (slice 6).
- Information questions: `이게 뭐예요?`, `화장실이 어디예요?`, `지금 몇 시예요?`, `이름이 뭐예요?` (slice 4).
- Detail confirmation: `이름은 민서예요?`, `이 번호가 맞아요?`, `여기가 서울역이에요?`, `약속은 세 시예요?` (slice 8).
- Compact requests: `써 주세요`, `보여 주세요`, `잠깐 기다려 주세요`, `확인해 주세요` (slice 9).
- Audio/readiness exchange: `들려요?` / `잘 안 들려요` and `준비됐어요?` / `네, 준비됐어요` (slice 10).
- Orthographic word groups used for connected sound: `한국어`, `한국말`, `국물`, `앞문`, `국밥`, `책상`, `축하`, `입학`, `실내`, and `물놀이` (slices 6–8). They remain spelled conventionally even when their connected pronunciation changes.

No chunk authorizes arbitrary noun, number, particle, ending, or verb replacement. A generator may reuse an earlier chunk as visible support, but it must not create several targets that differ only by a substituted name, number, or noun.

## 5. Pronunciation and orthography

- Establish direct sound-to-Hangul associations through words and short utterances, never through English sound labels.
- Cover all basic consonant letters, all five doubled/tense consonant letters, the major modern vowel letters, and the productive compound vowels listed in the script inventory.
- Give substantial contextual recognition and production practice to `ㄱ/ㅋ/ㄲ`, `ㄷ/ㅌ/ㄸ`, `ㅂ/ㅍ/ㅃ`, `ㅈ/ㅊ/ㅉ`, and `ㅅ/ㅆ`.
- Teach `ㅐ` and `ㅔ` as distinct spellings selected by the known word and sentence context. Do not require an audio-only contrast. Apply the same fairness rule to `ㅚ`, `ㅙ`, and `ㅞ` where contemporary pronunciation overlaps.
- Cover single 받침 spellings and the seven broad citation-final realization families without implying that the shared sound identifies a unique spelling.
- Cover common complex 받침 only in the real words listed below. Do not extrapolate a full inflectional system from those forms.
- Contrast standard spelling with resyllabification, nasalization, tensification, aspiration involving `ㅎ`, `ㄴ/ㄹ` lateralization, and frequent cluster behavior. Answers always use standard orthography.
- Contrast careful word-focused delivery with ordinary connected delivery in complete phrases. Do not store pronunciation imitations as answers or alternatives.
- Teach phrase grouping and natural boundaries before final-contour discrimination. Questions that need a clear synthesis cue retain `?` in `sentence`.

## 6. Register

- Default register: contemporary ordinary polite Korean suitable for a new acquaintance, classroom, shop, café, station, or simple help interaction.
- `미안해요` is an ordinary polite personal apology; `죄송합니다` is a more deferential conventional apology. Cards must provide a setting that makes the choice natural.
- `감사합니다`, `처음 뵙겠습니다`, and `잘 부탁드립니다` are learned as conventional complete expressions. Their formal endings do not initiate speech-level conversion practice.
- `여기요` is limited to polite service attention in a suitable setting. It is not modeled as a shout or as a general substitute for a request.
- Do not generate casual intimate endings, command-like bare forms, elaborate business language, broad honorific morphology, slang, dialect, or character-like speech.

## 7. Pragmatics

- `네` can acknowledge that something was heard or accepted; it is not glossed as an unconditional logical yes in every context.
- `아니요`, `아니에요`, `안 돼요`, and `괜찮아요` receive distinct situations so that refusal, correction, prohibition/impossibility, and reassurance are not conflated.
- The two leave-taking expressions are selected by who leaves and who remains.
- Questions about an object, restroom, name, and time require a visible or described reason for asking; they are not decontextualized substitution drills.
- Repetition, slower-speech, writing, showing, waiting, checking, and help requests use a courteous tone and leave room for the other person to respond.
- Name, number, place, and time confirmation cards each use a different practical scene. The visible context and complete audio identify the target fairly.
- Statement, question, request, and confirmation contours are interpreted together with wording, punctuation, and situation; intonation is never the sole clue to an otherwise ambiguous spelling.
- Repair expressions are modeled as normal interaction management, not as criticism of the interlocutor.

## 8. Listening targets

- Segment a short utterance into syllable blocks and phrase groups without transliteration support (slices 1–10).
- Recognize the target word in a complete contextual utterance for each plain/aspirated/tense family and for `ㅅ/ㅆ` (slices 2–3, 10).
- Distinguish the high-value vowel contexts `ㅏ/ㅓ`, `ㅓ/ㅗ`, `ㅗ/ㅜ`, `ㅡ/ㅜ`, `ㅣ/ㅡ`, `ㅑ/ㅕ`, `ㅛ/ㅠ`, and `ㅘ/ㅝ` (slices 1–4, 10).
- Use lexical and sentence context, not sound alone, for `ㅐ/ㅔ` and overlapping `ㅚ/ㅙ/ㅞ` spellings (slices 2–4, 10).
- Recognize a word with a simple final at a phrase boundary while accepting that several spellings can share the same final realization (slice 5).
- Recognize resyllabification, nasalization, tensification, `ㅎ`-related aspiration, `ㄴ/ㄹ` lateralization, and common cluster behavior in the controlled word bank (slices 6–8, 10).
- Hear the difference between careful word-focused pronunciation and natural connected delivery without treating either as a different spelling (slices 5–10).
- Follow short phrase boundaries and distinguish a clear statement, yes/no question, information question, polite request, and confirmation check (slices 9–10).

## 9. Production targets

- Read unfamiliar blocks assembled from established letters, including vowel-initial blocks, doubled initials, compound medials, simple finals, and covered complex finals.
- Type dictated words and short expressions with the standard Korean two-set keyboard, correct a wrong component without retyping unrelated text, and preserve standard spacing and punctuation.
- Produce the five major onset contrast families in meaningful words or phrases with enough context to recover the intended word.
- Produce the listed vowel contrasts and compound-vowel words while preserving their standard spelling.
- Produce simple-final and covered complex-final words both carefully and within a short natural phrase.
- Produce the controlled examples of all required connected-sound families without altering the written form.
- Deliver the 40 indexed expressions in the stated pragmatic setting with ordinary polite rhythm and an appropriate final contour.
- Complete an integrated listen–type–read cycle for short Korean 00 utterances without romanization, phonetic respelling, or Korean 01 grammar support.

## 10. Prerequisites

- Entry prerequisite: none in Korean. Learners need only the ability to play the full card audio and activate a standard Korean two-set keyboard.
- No Latin transliteration, prior vocabulary, grammar terminology, Hanja, or knowledge of Korean letter names is assumed.
- Within Korean 00, the binding graph is a single cumulative chain:

```text
Slice 1 → Slice 2 → Slice 3 → Slice 4 → Slice 5
        → Slice 6 → Slice 7 → Slice 8 → Slice 9 → Slice 10
```

- Exact dependencies: slice 1 has none; slice 2 requires slice 1; slice 3 requires slices 1–2; slice 4 requires slices 1–3; slice 5 requires slices 1–4; slice 6 requires slices 1–5; slice 7 requires slices 1–6; slice 8 requires slices 1–7; slice 9 requires slices 1–8; slice 10 requires slices 1–9.
- A slice may contain a compact fixed expression with not-yet-analyzed spelling or sound behavior. That exception applies only to the expression itself; it cannot be used as evidence that the underlying pattern is already productive.

## 11. Exclusions

- No complete beginner grammar or proficiency-test syllabus; systematic tense/aspect, conjugation, negation, particle, copula, or connective instruction; indirect quotation; or connected narration.
- No complete native or Sino-Korean number system, counters, dates, prices, phone-number production, or time paradigm. `세 시` and one visible number occur only inside fixed confirmation contexts.
- No productive formal speech-level conversion, broad honorific morphology, elaborate business language, casual intimate register, slang, aggressive speech, dialect production, or strongly gendered or theatrical speech.
- No Hanja study, romanization, Latin transliteration prompts, phonetic respelling, English-equivalent consonant labels, or isolated letter-name trivia.
- No audio-only spelling decisions for neutralized finals, `ㅐ/ㅔ`, or potentially overlapping `ㅚ/ㅙ/ㅞ`; no isolated phonetics answer whose full utterance and context fail to identify the word.
- No rare complex-final drill set. `ㄽ`, `ㄾ`, and `ㄿ` are outside this stage; `ㄳ` receives only controlled awareness and contextual typing through `몫이`.
- No generalized phonology engine, exhaustive exception list, pronunciation scoring, accent imitation, ASR, forced alignment, or multiple-voice comparison.
- No Korean 01 vocabulary expansion merely to create examples. Each card must draw from the controlled lexical pool, an earlier accepted expression, or one compact expression introduced in its own slice.

## Slice plan and prerequisite graph

### Generation rules shared by all slices

- Generate exactly the stated IDs and counts; do not borrow an ID from another slice.
- Use only established earlier material, the controlled words assigned to the current slice, and the four compact fixed expressions introduced in that slice.
- Target the four indexed expressions once each. Reuse is permitted only as unharmed visible or audio context for a different target.
- Every sound contrast must be embedded in a complete meaningful utterance. Provide visible and semantic context that identifies the intended standard word.
- For a whole-utterance expression card, hiding the complete utterance is allowed because the expression is short and fixed. Otherwise hide only the focused word or compact chunk.
- Keep `accepted_answers` empty unless an independent editor documents a genuine standard alternative in meaning or spacing. Never accept a pronunciation imitation.
- Treat every subrange allocation as binding. Within a subrange, cover distinct targets rather than mechanical substitutions.
